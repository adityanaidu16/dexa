# Research: "Wafer" — identification of the AI infrastructure company (wafer.ai, formerly Herdora, YC S25) and any other plausible "Wafer" candidates; what it does, founding, team, funding, products, claimed numbers, and positioning vs. inference providers

## Summary

The company most consistent with "Wafer" as an AI-infrastructure startup is Wafer (wafer.ai), San Francisco, Y Combinator Summer 2025, founded 2025 by Emilio Andere (CEO) and Steven Arellano, both University of Chicago alumni. It launched under the name Herdora as "Cursor for CUDA" (an agent turning PyTorch into optimized GPU kernels, claiming 1.5–5x speedups) and later rebranded; the YC Herdora page, herdora.com, GitHub contact email and dual YC launch slugs confirm the link. From December 2025 to March 2026 it shipped developer tools (VS Code/Cursor profiling extension, Perfetto viewer, ROCprofiler, cloud CUDA compiler, GPU Workspaces on B200/MI300X, Trace Compare, KernelArena benchmark). From April 2026 it sold inference directly: Wafer Pass ($10/week Starter, $25/week Privacy with ZDR), an OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible serverless API at pass.wafer.ai (GLM-5.2/5.3, Kimi-K2.6/K3, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.5-397B), and dedicated endpoints; third-party trackers list GLM-5.2 at $1.20/$4.10 per 1M tokens and a "Wafer Fast" tier at $3.00/$10.25. The company's technology page describes agents that profile, explore and deploy configurations across batching, decoding, quantization, engines (vLLM, SGLang), kernels and hardware (NVIDIA B200/B300, AMD MI350X/MI355X, AWS Trainium). Published claims include GLM-5.2 on MI355X at 2,626 tok/s/node (~80% of B200 at >2x lower GPU cost), Kimi K3 at 48 vs 33 tok/s/$ versus B300, Qwen3.6-35B at ~15,000 tok/s/node, DigitalOcean's Kimi 2.5 at 11.33x baseline, an upstreamed AMD AITER kernel, "2T+ tokens of continual inference," and testimonials from Neon Health, Brilliant and YC's Jared Friedman ("crushing Cerebras on latency"). Funding: $4M seed led by Fifty Years (April 14, 2026, with Liquid2, YC, angels including Jeff Dean and Wojciech Zaremba) and a $40M Series A co-led by Marathon and Chemistry with Wing, AMD Ventures and Outset Capital (Sept 1, 2026); The Information's headline reports a $200M-plus valuation and acquisition offers, with secondary coverage saying offers came from cloud/inference providers and were declined. Positioning statements emphasize faster execution of models teams already want rather than catalog breadth, ZDR as a default option, and the thesis that software optimization can make non-Nvidia hardware competitive. No other AI-inference company named Wafer was found; other "Wafer" entities are semiconductor/IT firms, and "wafer-scale" references point to Cerebras. ARR, valuation specifics, acquirer names and dedicated pricing remain undisclosed.
