# Research: Morph LLM (morphllm.com) — products, performance claims, pricing, customers/integrations, team/funding, inference stack, benchmarks, origin story, and public commentary on the specialized-model + custom-inference playbook

## Summary

Morph (legal entity AutoInfra, Inc.; YC S23) is a San Francisco company founded by Tejas Bhakta, an ex-Tesla ML/inference engineer. Per his own account, the original YC S23 product was AutoInfra (AI for AWS); after a period of pivots he began the current product around February 2025 after studying why Cursor's fast-apply felt fast, and launched in mid-2025. Product Hunt/YC launched Fast Apply at 1,600+ tok/s (May 2025), Launch HN at 4,500+ tok/s (July 2025), and the company now claims 10,500 tok/s at 98% accuracy on B200s via custom CUDA kernels and a task-specific speculative decoder.

The catalog today: Fast Apply (morph-v3-fast/large; $0.80/$1.20 and $0.90/$1.90 per M tokens), WarpGrep (an RL-trained parallel code-search subagent, $0.80/M, claimed #1 on SWE-Bench Pro with +2 to +3.7 point lifts for Opus 4.6/GPT-5.3 Codex/MiniMax 2.5), Compact/FlashCompact (33,000 tok/s line-deletion compaction, $0.20/$0.50), Reflexes (multi-head trace classifiers, ~90ms, $0.001/event, engine 'forked from vLLM' with custom kernels), Router ($0.005/request), Glance (computer-use testing), embeddings/rerank v4, and hosted open-weight models (Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4 Flash) with 1M context, prefix caching, sticky KV placement, FP4 kernels, and a half-price 'standby' tier. Distribution is OpenAI/Anthropic-compatible APIs, MCP, Vercel AI SDK, OpenRouter, plugins for OpenCode and Claude Code, and a GitHub App.

Named users (founder-stated): create.xyz, databutton, continue.dev, Framer, Webflow, Block, Vercel. Self-reported traction: $1.1M revenue in five months (founder blog), '$6M run rate as a solo dev', '$7M run rate adding $1M/week' and '0 → 10M revenue as a 1-person company' (employee #1 tweet, Aug 25 2026; YC job post). YC lists team size 3. No funding beyond YC is documented in primary sources; aggregator data conflicts. The founder frames the playbook as: pick a narrow, repetitive agent workload whose output is highly predictable, train a small specialized model plus a specialized speculator, and 'almost make our own inference engine just for this task.' Peers (Relace, Cognition/SWE-grep on Cerebras) publish similar specialized-model-plus-fast-inference approaches; one June 2025 essay argued fast-apply models have a short shelf life, which the founder publicly disputed.
