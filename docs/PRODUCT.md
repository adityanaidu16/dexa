## 0. How to read this

**The rule this document follows.** It does not decide that a market is too small, and it does not decide that a direction is dead because someone else ships something adjacent. Those judgments are yours. Where such a judgment would normally appear, the document states the facts on both sides and files the judgment under *Founder decisions* (section 7) with the evidence that would inform it.

**What it does decide.** Whether a number is measured, modeled, bounded, contradicted, or merely designed; what an honest proof-of-value benchmark for each direction would look like; what exists in code today; and what the first six weeks of building would prove.

**Status vocabulary** (used for every measured claim):

| label | meaning |
|---|---|
| PROVEN | measured, with setup recorded, usually reproduced more than one way |
| BOUNDED | measured, but a ceiling or a strong caveat limits the claim |
| CONTRADICTED | two measurements in the repos point different ways; both are cited |
| FALSIFIED | the thesis as stated was tested and failed |
| OPEN | designed or planned, not measured |

## 1. The short version

**What the experiments established.** Across roughly thirty tested theses, most inference-efficiency ideas measured on real GPUs collapsed to about 2x, to a configuration change any customer can copy, or to a wall. Two mechanisms held up as *capabilities* rather than speedups, and one held up as a capacity win:

- **Restoring a session's KV cache from pinned CPU memory beats re-computing it**, by 10x at 4k tokens and 17x at 16k inside a real vLLM stack, single request (PROVEN); no in-engine restore has been measured above 16k. The same claim measured through a disk-backed store under concurrency **lost** to vLLM's batched re-prefill (0.37x at 8k). The reconciliation is arithmetic: restore wins when the tier's bandwidth times the engine's prefill time exceeds the KV bytes. Pinned RAM at 10 to 20 GB/s wins from about 4k tokens; a disk path under 1 GB/s wins only near 64k. **Nobody has measured the RAM tier at 16k to 64k under concurrency**, which is exactly the regime a long-context agent product would live in.
- **A session's KV state can be saved by one vLLM process and resumed by another with prefill skipped** (PROVEN at tensor-parallel size 1, but thin: identical output is recorded on a 125M-parameter model over two blocks, and 8,176 of 8,192 tokens on a 0.6B model at 8k; the lossless 8k-to-64k result on an 8B model is HuggingFace-level, not inside the serving engine). That is a durability and portability property, not a latency property.
- **Telling the serving engine when a session will return moved a concurrency cliff.** On one L40S with a 7B model, baseline vLLM served 200 concurrent voice sessions inside a 300 ms p95 first-token budget and then collapsed. Pinning sessions predicted to return within 25 seconds, and letting the rest evict into a fast restore path, held 300 (1.5x). Single seed, synthetic traces, 46 GB GPU, and below the 2x line the experiment pre-registered (BOUNDED).

**What the outside world looks like today** (facts, sources in section 4): every shipping KV-cache layer evicts with an LRU-family policy that does not know when a session will return; the SGLang proposals that would carry such hints are open or inactive; no first-party provider sells guaranteed session-pinned KV; frontier caches expire in 5 minutes to 24 hours; voice platforms price by concurrent line and one of them states it over-provisions GPU headroom to avoid queueing; production coding-agent traces show median prefixes of 115k to 126k tokens and human gaps with a median of 1.4 minutes and a p90 of 20.6 minutes. Morph reached a reported seven-figure run rate as a one-person company by training a narrow model and building its own engine for it; Wafer went from a kernel-optimization tool to an AMD-based inference provider with a $40M Series A in fourteen months.

**The eleven candidates.** Section 5 fleshes out each: (A) a session-stateful provider for long-idle agents; (B) conversation-native voice inference sold on sessions-per-GPU; (C) one session-aware residency scheduler for every idle regime, sold hosted, BYOC, and as engagements; (D) a document-VLM provider; (E) verifier-guided search inference for code; (F) a portable, governed KV-state layer as open-core infrastructure; (G) cartridges, a context compiler; (H) a computer-use agent endpoint; (I) the Morph playbook applied to document parsing; (J) preemptible-GPU inference made safe by portable state; (K) a benchmark-first "sessions-per-GPU" standard plus a resident-slot SLA.

**What they have in common.** Seven of the eleven (A, B, C, F, J, K, and the session half of H) share one mechanism: a KV connector that owns residency decisions the engine's LRU cannot make. They differ in workload, customer, and business shape, not in the core code. The other four (D, E, G, I) are model- or scheduler-side bets with different evidence profiles.

**The decisions only you can make** are listed in section 7. The ones that change the build order most: voice-first or agent-first; hosted endpoint, BYOC software, or the Wafer-style engagement motion; whether adjacency from Tensormesh, llm-d, NVIDIA Dynamo, and the SGLang RFCs is a threat or a set of baselines; and which GPU class the proof runs on, because both repos name "a bigger-HBM GPU moves the wall" as their top unmeasured threat.

**What verification changed.** Each brief was fact-checked against the ledgers and attacked by a separate pass. Final grades: nine candidates at C (A, B, C, D, E, F, I, J, K) and two at D (G, H). Nothing reached B, because every in-engine measurement is single-seed on one GPU class and, in each case, the mechanism the product would sell is the part that was never run. Four findings recur: the baselines are stale (no candidate has been measured against vLLM's in-tree OffloadingConnector, LMCache 0.5.x, SGLang HiCache, or FP8 KV, any of which could erase the margins); the 80 GB GPU question is unmeasured everywhere it matters; no design partner is named in any file, so every six-week plan that assumed one was re-labeled; and the verifiers' estimates for a proof a skeptical buyer could rerun are 7 to 14 weeks, not the six the drafts assumed. Two drafts also carried errors the pass removed: a fabricated claim that L40S pricing was absent from the research, and a 2.12x wall-clock figure whose two arms verified asymmetrically.

**The experiments that inform the most paths at once** (section 8): the restore-versus-re-prefill crossover surface (context by concurrency by tier) on one model and one GPU; the same voice sweep on an 80 GB GPU; and a three-seed replication of the 1.5x. Roughly 60 to 100 GPU-hours in total.

This section digests the two evidence ledgers. Setups are stated because they bound what can be claimed. Full entries with sources are in `docs/product/context/01-*.md` and `02-*.md`.

### 2.1 Efficiency theses that collapsed or hit a wall (dexa repo)

### 2.2 The stateful-session thesis (dexa repo)

| measurement | number | setup |
|---|---|---|
| Restore vs. re-prefill, raw tensor copies | 11.8x at 4k, 11.5x at 16k, 17.4x at 32k, 28.9x at 64k | Qwen2.5-7B, A100-80GB, HuggingFace tensors, single request. Note: the re-prefill side is HF eager prefill, several times slower than vLLM's |

| Cross-instance resume | Two separate vLLM processes; the second loads the first's KV and skips prefill. Identical output is recorded on OPT-125m (two blocks); 8,176 of 8,192 tokens matched on Qwen3-0.6B at 8k (next-token correct); the bit-identical 8k to 64k result on Llama-3.1-8B is HF-level | TP=1, single attention backend, single-step prefill only. Chunked prefill is not saved; decode-token KV is never saved; TP>1 is not implemented |
| Independent benchmark (`vllm bench serve`, prefix sharing) | The connector was 5.5x slower than a no-cache baseline because it keys on the whole prompt and gets zero hits on shared prefixes | OPT-125m, A10G |

**The contradiction and its resolution** (conflict 1 in `04-conflicts.md`). The 10 to 34x and the 0.37x are both real. Written next to each number, the restore tier's effective bandwidth explains everything: pinned RAM at 10 to 20 GB/s crosses over below 4k tokens; a Modal-volume disk path at under 1 GB/s crosses over near 64k. What no run in either repo measured: the RAM tier at 16k to 64k under concurrent load, on the same model and GPU. That single experiment is the pivot for candidates A, C, F, J, and K.

**Two more things the ledger insists on.** First, of the "three independent reproductions" the findings memo cites, one is vLLM's own prefix cache and one is stock LMCache; the claimed delta over LMCache, the economic tiering policy, is designed, modeled, and not enforced anywhere in the code. Second, restore evidence above 16k inside a production engine does not exist; the 32k and 64k restore points are HF-level, and the README's "100k+ token coding agent" workload has never been measured as a restore in-engine (the engine did prefill Llama-3.1-8B at 128k in the branching runs, at 32.7 s per prefill).

### 2.3 The voice experiment (voice-inference repo)

Rig: Modal L40S (46 GB), vLLM 0.9.2, Qwen2.5-7B fp16, synthetic conversation traces with 10% barge-ins, seed 1, real time. Metric: maximum concurrent sessions at p95 first-token latency at or below 300 ms.

| arm | mechanism | max passing N | p95 at N=300 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: vLLM with prefix caching | HBM only | 200 | collapse (prefix-hit rate fell from 92% to under 2%) |
| B1: LMCache 0.3.2 | reactive CPU tiering, chunked loads | 200 | 26.0 s (6.0 s with layerwise loading) |
| B2: custom connector | same strategy, contiguous session-granular transfers through a pinned slab | 200 | 316.8 ms, stable, 17 ms over the gate |
| C: audio-triggered prefetch | warmup request fired at speech onset | 200 | 15.0 s (a collapse; the server also died at minute 41 of that run, so 37% of turns have no recorded latency) |
| D: predictive pinning | each request carries its predicted idle window; short-idle sessions pinned in HBM | **300** | **272.2 ms** |

What the ledger adds to the memo you already have:

- The 1.5x is one seed, one GPU, one passing point, and it is 300 divided by 200: no N between 200 and 300 or between 300 and 400 was run, so the true ratio is bracketed somewhere in [1.0x, 2.0x). Over the full-load window (minutes 5 to 40) the p95 is 291.9 ms, not 272.2 ms, and 3 of 7 five-minute buckets exceeded 300 ms. N=400 failed within ten minutes.
- The only offload baseline measured is LMCache 0.3.2 on vLLM 0.9.2. vLLM's native OffloadingConnector (in-tree since 0.11, LRU or ARC), LMCache 0.5.x with layerwise loading, and SGLang HiCache were never run, so the comparison does not yet isolate the pinning policy from a stale baseline.
- The idle-window hint is computed from the synthetic trace's own median constants, and the 25-second pin threshold pins essentially every reply of 76 tokens or fewer (the median reply is 75). Whether the gain comes from prediction or from "pin every short reply" needs a shuffled-hint ablation, which was not run.
- Restores run synchronously inside the scheduler step and already cost about 24 ms of p95 at N=200 with 140 MB sessions on a 7B model; per-restore timings were never captured. A code-reading finding (a store evicts the slab entry it just matched, so a new session's first turn can evict a sibling sharing its system prompt) may have depressed the recorded 35 to 52% hit rates.
- The mechanism the original plan specified, freeing idle sessions' HBM at turn end to bound the working set, was never built. What was built and measured is cheap eviction plus selective pinning. The stock KV-connector API can delay a block's free (pin) and manage the connector's own tier, but it cannot order HBM eviction or prefetch without a request; proactive turn-end offload, demotion, and "enforced tiering" therefore need either vLLM's OffloadingConnector policy hook, the proposed TieringManager, or a fork.
- The whole-run p95 for the connector without hints was 316.8 ms; on the full-load window it was 336.8 ms. The arm-D advantage at N=300 is 44.6 ms of p95 over the same connector without hints, which makes the 1.5x an SLO-threshold artifact in one specific sense: at any SLO between 350 and 400 ms both configurations hold N=300 and the ratio is 1.0x. A published SLO curve, rather than a single 300 ms number, is what would show where the policy matters.
- Pinning holds about 70 sessions inside the 10 GB HBM budget; every retained session beyond that rides the RAM-tier restore path, which is the configuration that failed the 300 ms gate at N=300 without hints. A retention guarantee at scale therefore rests on the arm that failed, not the arm that passed.
- The audio stream contributed nothing measurable in any arm; the pinning signal is response length plus turn-taking statistics.
- The "7 ms restore" is a docstring estimate; no per-restore timings were archived. Server-side hit rates quoted in the write-up are not archived either.
- The 95% idle figure comes from synthetic traces computed with a different model's KV geometry and a decode speed higher than the one measured on the GPU.
- Both repos name the same top threat and neither measured it: on an 80 GB GPU the baseline's wall likely moves past voice-realistic session counts. The arithmetic in `04-conflicts.md` puts the baseline cliff at roughly 400 to 450 sessions on an 80 GB part for these traces.

### 2.4 Claims that must not be cited

The superseded-claims list at the end of `04-conflicts.md` has 27 entries. The ones most likely to leak into a pitch: "14 to 25x faster resume" (CPU, tiny model); "3.7 to 5.5x faster than re-prefill" (versus HF eager, not vLLM); "latency win is unconditional" (single request only); "40x cheaper than GPT-4o" (modeled); "7.3x fewer visual tokens" (superseded by the 2.3x ceiling); "the thesis is a business" (the differentiating policy is unmeasured); "PROCEED_3X" (the measured outcome was 1.5x with the tested mechanism at 1.0x).

## 4. The outside world, as facts

Digest of the nine research files. Confidence and URLs are in `05-research-*.md`.

### 4.1 The two playbooks you named

**Morph** (`05-research-morph.md`). Legal entity AutoInfra, YC S23; the current product started in February 2025 after the founder studied why Cursor's fast-apply felt fast. Fast Apply merges code edits at a claimed 10,500 tokens per second and 98% accuracy on B200s using custom CUDA kernels and a task-shaped speculative decoder ("roughly 70 or 80% of the content is almost exactly the same, so you're essentially using the original code as a guess"; "we're almost making our own inference engine just for this task"). The catalog grew by adding narrow endpoints: WarpGrep (an RL-trained code-search subagent), Compact (33,000 tokens per second line-deletion compaction), Reflexes (multi-head trace classifiers on a vLLM fork with decode removed), a Router, and hosted open models with sticky KV placement and a half-price standby tier. Self-reported traction: $1.1M in five months, then a $6M to $7M run rate as a solo founder, and "0 to 10M of revenue" as a one-person company per a job post; team size 3; no funding beyond YC in primary sources. Peers Relace and Cognition publish the same recipe (LoRA fine-tune of a 3 to 8B base, FP8, speculative decoding, RL-trained search subagents on fast hardware).

**Wafer** (`05-research-wafer.md`). YC S25, founded 2025 as Herdora ("Cursor for CUDA"). It pivoted from a kernel-optimization agent and profiling tools to a hosted inference provider on AMD MI355X in about a year: OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible serverless, dedicated endpoints with continual post-deployment optimization, and optimization engagements for clouds (DigitalOcean, Parasail). $4M seed in April 2026, $40M Series A on 2026-09-01 (Marathon, Chemistry, AMD Ventures; The Information reports a $200M+ valuation and acquisition offers). Eight people on the team page. An unlabeled logo carousel on its homepage shows Vapi, Tavus, Inworld, Vercel, and Ollama; whether they are customers, partners, or channels is not stated. Its stated thesis: GPU utilization averages about 20%, a few hundred people can hand-optimize accelerators, and "Nvidia's dominance is about software."

### 4.2 Generalized providers and their caches

Fireworks, Together, and Baseten publish proprietary or heavily modified engines (FireAttention kernels; Together's Turbo engine with speculators; Baseten's TensorRT-LLM builder and a vLLM-backed MoE stack with KV-aware routing). DeepInfra, Novita, Hyperbolic, Nebius, Parasail, RunPod, and Modal do not name a production engine. Fireworks closed a $1.5B Series D at $17.5B in July 2026 and states that over 95% of its tokens come from models specialized on customer data. Caching: Fireworks is automatic with a 50% discount, retention "several minutes up to several hours", and session-affinity headers because the cache lives in one replica; Groq expires after 2 hours; Cerebras guarantees 5 minutes with no discount; DeepInfra sells explicit 5-minute or 1-hour retention at 1.25x or 2x the input price to write. No provider surveyed sells guaranteed session-pinned KV. Retention on open-model providers is long enough to cover most measured agent gaps (OpenAI 24 hours on earlier models, DeepSeek hours to days, Fireworks up to several hours, Groq 2 hours, Cerebras up to 1 hour, DeepInfra 1 hour explicit); the documented cache-miss pain is Anthropic's 5-minute default and Azure/OpenAI in-memory caches. Time-bounded retention also exists in the open stores: Mooncake soft-pins with a TTL, Dynamo's KVBM exposes cache-control-style TTL semantics, and Morph offers per-request TTL control; what none of them has is eviction ordered by a predicted return time.

### 4.3 Frontier labs

Anthropic: 5-minute default TTL, 1-hour option at 2x write cost, reads at 0.1x; a community audit attributed a 17% cost increase in Claude Code to a switch from 1-hour to 5-minute writes. OpenAI: 30-minute minimum on GPT-5.6+, 24-hour retention on earlier models (KV offloaded to GPU-local storage), and `prompt_cache_key` that "influences routing but does not pin." Gemini: explicit caches billed per token-hour of storage ($0.50 to $4.50 per million tokens per hour). DeepSeek: disk cache cleared "within a few hours to a few days." Anthropic's Managed Agents bill $0.08 per session-hour while running and nothing while idle, the first time-priced session primitive in the survey.

### 4.4 The KV-cache infrastructure ecosystem

LMCache (Apache-2.0, 11.6k stars) and its company Tensormesh ($24.5M raised; SaaS in beta billing cached input at $0; an on-prem Operator "coming soon"; post-v1 pricing 30% of estimated savings). Mooncake Store (approximate LRU, 10-second leases, soft pins up to 24 hours; a draft PR for TTL-bounded retention leases). NVIDIA Dynamo's KVBM (four tiers, presence/LFU offload filters; a BlueField-4 flash tier announced for 2H 2026). llm-d (CNCF sandbox; precise KV-event routing, tiered offload via vLLM's native OffloadingConnector with LRU or ARC, P2P KV pull, session affinity; powers GKE Inference Gateway). SGLang HiCache (private per-instance host memory; two RFCs for agent-aware and programmatic KV hints with pin, demote, prefetch, and TTL, both open or inactive). Production trace studies: Alibaba's ideal hit ratios 54 to 62% with a p99 KV lifespan of 97 seconds; llm-d's 219 Claude Code sessions with 96% of requests reusing at least 90% of their input verbatim and inter-turn pauses from 2 seconds median to 11.4 minutes p99. The Continuum paper pins GPU KV with a TTL derived from reload cost and reports over 8x job-completion improvements on agent benchmarks. **No shipping system in the survey evicts on predicted return time.**

### 4.5 Voice AI infrastructure

Three sourcing patterns: pass-through to frontier APIs (Vapi, Retell, Pipecat), self-hosted open models (LiveKit serves Gemma 4 31B on SGLang at 192 ms TTFT and states it reserves "more headroom than a throughput-maximized deployment would"; Inworld, ElevenLabs, Deepgram host 26B to 397B open models), and speech-native models. Concurrency is priced per line (Vapi $10 per line per month beyond 10; Retell $8 beyond 20). Volumes: Vapi 1 to 5 million calls per day; ElevenLabs 10 million conversations per week. The LLM's share of a voice minute ranges from under 5% (Inworld's self-hosted model) to about 65% (Retell's frontier lines). Latency budgets put LLM first-token at 375 ms target and 750 ms maximum. Nobody publishes sessions-per-GPU at a first-token SLO for the LLM stage, although adjacent public agent-trace benchmarks exist (vLLM's Mooncake trace replay, llm-d's Claude Code traces, VAST and Backend.AI's long-context runs); hosted endpoints run through the same client would yield a knee at requests-per-minute with an unknown GPU count behind it, a different quantity.

### 4.6 Long-running agents

TraceLab (4,300 Claude Code and Codex sessions): median prefix 115k to 126k tokens per step, ~860 new tokens, human gaps median 1.4 minutes and p90 20.6 minutes, tool gaps 5.2 seconds median and 81 seconds p99, 95.7% prefix-cache hits overall but 84.4% on human-initiated steps. Across every published gap distribution (TraceLab, llm-d's 2-second median and 11.4-minute p99, SJTU/Alibaba's 80% of reuses within 10 minutes and 97-second p99 KV lifespan), hour-scale idle is the p90 to p99 tail, not the body. Anthropic: the 99.9th-percentile Claude Code turn grew from under 25 to over 45 minutes. A Codex user on Azure saw total cache misses past 150k tokens and 71% of input re-billed. Durable-execution runtimes (Temporal, Inngest, LangGraph) wait indefinitely at no compute cost; the model-side cache is what expires. Open-weight models carried a majority of OpenRouter tokens by mid-2026, concentrated in coding and agents; Cursor's Composer 2 started from Kimi K2.5; OpenHands recommends Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on vLLM or SGLang with a 22k-token minimum and a 32k context window, which is the only open-weight agent context guidance in the research files; the 115k to 195k prefixes come from Claude Code and Codex traces on frontier models.

### 4.7 Document AI

Gemini bills a PDF page at 560 tokens (about $0.42 per thousand pages of input). DeepSeek's V4-flash-vision (2026-08-21) caps an image at 384 tokens at V4-Flash prices, about $0.17 per thousand images at peak and $0.08 off-peak (derived, accuracy unmeasured); gpt-5.6-luna lands near $0.25 per thousand pages. ## 5. The eleven candidates

Each candidate has a full brief under `docs/product/briefs/` written to one template (thesis, customer, pain, proof benchmark, architecture, evidence, six-week plan, pricing, competitive facts, kill gates, founder decisions, combinations) and then fact-checked against the ledgers and attacked by a skeptic. This section is the digest. Evidence grades: **A** core claim reproduced several ways in-engine; **B** measured once in-engine or at the physics level; **C** measured but bounded or contradicted; **D** mostly design.

### A. Session-stateful inference provider for long-idle, long-context agents

**Thesis.** A durable, warm session is the primitive. Its KV is tiered HBM to RAM to NVMe by measured break-evens and priced as residency plus new tokens, so a coding or computer-use agent that idles past a provider's cache TTL restores instead of re-paying its 100k-token prefix. Sold as a hosted OpenAI-compatible endpoint with one optional `session` field.

**Evidence.** Grade C, confirmed by the verifier. Restore beats re-prefill in a real vLLM+LMCache stack single-request (10x at 4k, 17x at 16k) on Qwen2.5-7B, and in-engine restore evidence stops at 16k; the RAM tier under concurrency at 16k to 64k is unmeasured (the only concurrent RAM-tier data is 2.5k-token voice sessions on an L40S); the tiering policy that is the claimed delta over LMCache is advisory in every deployed path, and nothing in either repo demotes or frees KV proactively, so the differentiator (client-hinted, tier-spanning demotion beating stock LRU or ARC offload at agent context under serving concurrency) would grade D on its own. The resident-set arithmetic moves the agent-scale cells to NVMe: 32 sessions at 64k on Llama-3.1-8B is 269 GB, above most single-GPU hosts' RAM, and NVMe restore has only been measured as a naive load at about 3 GB/s single-request. When RAM is plentiful an LRU never evicts, so the policy's effect is GB-hours, worth roughly $0.06 to $0.13 per 128k-session-hour against $0.007 to $0.023 for the re-prefill it avoids on a 7 to 8B model, which makes the dollar case thin at this model size and the case, if any, a capacity and p99 case. The pain assumption (gaps exceed retention) is documented for Anthropic's 5-minute default and Azure/OpenAI in-memory caches, not for open-model providers, whose retention runs from 1 hour to days. The customer telemetry is strong (median 115k to 126k prefixes on frontier-model traces, 1.4-minute median human gaps) but is not open-weight telemetry. The verifier's estimate is 6 to 7 weeks to an A100-only benchmark (16k to 64k, three seeds, burst arrivals, correctness archived) and 9 to 12 weeks for the full grid with an H100 point, 128k, and NVMe.

**Proof benchmark.** Warm-resume p99 TTFT, GPU-seconds per turn, and resident GB-hours on 16k to 64k sessions (128k in the full grid) across an idle ladder (10 s, 2 min, 20 min, 2 h) at 8 to 32 concurrent sessions with burst arrivals, with the primary comparison against the strongest OSS offload (vLLM's OffloadingConnector with LRU and ARC, LMCache 0.5.x layerwise) at matched memory, and vanilla vLLM only as a sanity arm: at 64k by 32 vanilla's HBM cannot hold the working set, so it re-prefills by construction and any CPU offload beats it. Correctness is bitwise KV-block equality plus top-1 and KL agreement against a cold-prefill reference, not greedy text identity, which the voice repo's own probe found is the wrong test. This is the crossover-surface experiment from `04-conflicts.md`, with the per-cell RAM budget stated.

**Reused.** The session API and tier calculator in `dexa_platform/sessions`, the gateway and control plane, `serve/vllm_lmcache_backend.py`, the LMCache and warmstart eval scripts. **New:** enforced tiering, NVMe tier, chunked-prefill persistence, streaming, a session-to-replica router.

**Kill gate.** Session-aware tiering fails to beat the best OSS offload by 15% on p99 TTFT under burst or on GB-hours at equal p99 at matched memory (the verifier rates the original vanilla-vLLM gate fatal, because it measures the value of any CPU offload); or restore from RAM loses to re-prefill at 64k under 32-way concurrency; or the hint carries no measurable delta over a shuffled hint on real agent traces; or an H100 point shrinks both the latency ratio and the warm break-even past the product's price.

**Decisions it forces.** Voice-first or agent-first; hosted or BYOC; which differentiator to prove first (transfer-path robustness under burst, hinted tier demotion, or the session API and metering), since each has a different first experiment; which OSS offload is "the" baseline; whether the buyable unit is capacity and p99 or dollars, given the thin dollar case at 7 to 8B; which GPU class and which model class (7 to 8B dense at TP=1, where all the code is, or a partner's MoE or MLA model at TP=8 where no connector code exists); whether to upstream the hint protocol to SGLang's RFC, vLLM's TieringManager RFC, or Mooncake and sell only the hosted policy; the correctness contract sold to customers; whether a $100 provider-TTL probe (which proves the pain, not the value) is the first public artifact; the pre-registered thresholds themselves; single-replica or an affinity router; whether Tensormesh's $0-cached-input SaaS and the SGLang RFCs are baselines or a closing window.

### B. Conversation-native inference for voice agents

**Thesis.** The product metric is concurrent voice sessions per GPU at p95 first-token latency under 300 ms. The mechanism is the voice repo's arm D: each request carries its predicted idle window, short-idle sessions are pinned in HBM, long-idle ones evict into a contiguous pinned-RAM restore path. A vLLM connector plugin plus a session gateway; no fork. Sold to voice platforms that self-host open models (LiveKit, Inworld, ElevenLabs, Deepgram) and to their customers, hosted and BYOC.

**Evidence.** Grade C, confirmed by the verifier. The only configuration of five that moved the concurrency knee (200 to 300 on an L40S, 1.5x, with no intermediate N so the true ratio sits in [1.0x, 2.0x)); single seed; synthetic traces whose idle fraction comes from assumed distributions; 46 GB GPU; the full-load-window p95 was 292 ms with an 8 ms margin; the baselines are vanilla vLLM and a 2025 LMCache, not vLLM's native OffloadingConnector or LMCache 0.5.x; the hint is derived from the trace generator's own constants, so a shuffled-hint ablation is needed to show that prediction, rather than pinning every short reply, is what moved the knee; restores are synchronous and their latency was never timed; the connector is TP=1, fp16, non-MLA, prompt-KV only, while the platforms' documented defaults are 26 to 35B models. The repo's own thresholds conflict (README: under 2x is the kill band; RESULTS: 1.5x is above the arm-B gate); both readings are stated, neither is adopted here. LiveKit's statement that it over-provisions headroom and the per-line pricing at Vapi and Retell are the demand-side facts; no vendor page fetched advertises sessions per GPU, but that search was incomplete. The verifier's estimate of the first proof a skeptical buyer would accept (three seeds, H100, a 26 to 35B model, modern baselines, one real-transcript set, raw logs) is 10 to 14 weeks; the six-week plan below is the build schedule.

**Proof benchmark.** Max passing N at full-load-window p95 under 300 ms, median of three seeds, runs of at least 40 minutes (collapse was depth-triggered at minute 15 to 20), on L40S and on an 80 GB GPU, with a 26 to 35B model, against vanilla vLLM 0.28, vLLM's native OffloadingConnector (LRU and ARC), LMCache 0.5.4 layerwise, and SGLang HiCache, with a shuffled-hint ablation, on transcript-derived traces, with turns that never produced a first token counted as failures. Every Modal manifest today records the git SHA as unknown and no manifest names the connector or flags used; provenance is part of the deliverable.

**Reused.** `voice_kv_connector.py` (the pinning logic is about 40 lines plus a 6-line client hint), the ~2,000-line load harness, the sweep driver, the gateway. **New:** port from vLLM 0.9.2 to 0.28, async loads, proactive free of long-idle sessions, real-transcript converter, session-to-replica affinity, streaming and WebSocket transports.

**Kill gate.** Median D/A under 1.3x on three seeds; or the baseline cliff on an 80 GB GPU sits past voice-realistic session counts with D adding under 10% (the verifier rates this attack fatal if it lands; it is a week-1, roughly 10 GPU-hour test on the existing code); or a shuffled-hint arm matches D; or a modern stock tier (OffloadingConnector, LMCache 0.5.x) matches D; or real transcripts show idle fractions under 40%.

**Decisions it forces.** Cost product or latency product (the LLM is under 5% of a voice minute on some stacks and 65% on others); platforms or their customers as the buyer; whether to fork vLLM for scheduler-native prefetch; the speech-to-speech bet (continuous audio may collapse the idle window; unmeasured); the SLA number itself (300 ms p95 is the repo PRD's choice, while Twilio's published budget is 375 ms target and 750 ms maximum, and a looser SLA moves every knee); the ratio worth building on (2x pre-registered, 1.5x measured, and the repo's two thresholds disagree); vLLM-first or SGLang-first given that LiveKit, the named platform buyer, runs SGLang and SGLang's pin-with-TTL RFC is in flight; whether to run the cheap lethal tests (H100 arm A, stock OffloadingConnector) before or after the connector port; whether a design partner is a prerequisite, since none is named in any file.

### C. One session-aware residency scheduler for every idle regime

**Thesis.** The claim that unifies both repos: a scheduler that knows session semantics (predicted return time, context size, per-tier break-evens, workflow structure) beats LRU across idle windows from seconds (voice turns) to hours (human-in-the-loop agents). Built as a vLLM/SGLang connector, a policy engine, a published hint protocol, and a session router. Sold three ways: a hosted session-addressed endpoint, BYOC software for platforms, and optimization engagements for providers (the Wafer motion). The hint protocol is unowned: every shipping KV layer evicts by LRU, and the SGLang RFCs that would carry hints are open or inactive.

**Evidence.** Grade C, confirmed by the verifier. Same measured base as A and B (one voice win at 1.5x single seed, an 8 ms full-load margin, 44.6 ms of p95 over the same connector without hints; RAM restore 10 to 17x single-request, which is LMCache's mechanism); the agent-regime policy-versus-LRU comparison has never been run; no in-engine restore exists above 16k, which the verifier rates fatal for the agent half until a native-128k model restores in-engine; the hour-scale idle regime the session-hour pricing monetizes is the p90 to p99 tail of every measured gap distribution; NVMe is unmeasured; the cost model is HF-level and modeled; the stock connector API cannot express HBM eviction ordering or request-less prefetch, so "enforced tiering" needs vLLM's OffloadingConnector policy hook, the proposed TieringManager, or a fork; the SGLang path is unbuilt and SGLang's storage interface has no pin hook. DeepInfra's explicit 5-minute and 1-hour retention pricing is the closest shipped analogue to time-priced residency. The verifier's estimate is 8 to 10 weeks to a credible voice proof and 12 to 14 weeks, or unknown, for the agent proof.

**Proof benchmark.** Two metrics. Metric A is B's sessions-per-GPU sweep. Metric B is A's idle ladder at 100k-token scale. Ship target: 2x on three seeds for A; a 25% improvement over LMCache LRU on TTFT p95 or GPU-seconds for B. Kill: under 1.3x and under 15%.

**Reused.** Everything A and B reuse, plus the dexa connector's async-load and adaptive-policy code and the cost model as the seed of the policy engine. **New:** the enforced policy engine, the hint spec, the router (as an llm-d endpoint-picker plugin for BYOC), NVMe, server-metric capture.

**Kill gate.** Session-aware residency beats the best modern OSS tier (vLLM's OffloadingConnector with LRU or ARC, LMCache 0.5.x, FP8 KV) by under 1.15x; or it wins only on synthetic traces; or a native-128k model cannot restore a 100k session in-engine by week 4; or the connector API drifts faster than one engineer-week per release; or NVMe restore is over 3x slower than modeled.

**Decisions it forces.** Which of the three sales motions comes first, and whether three SKUs at once is one company or three; vLLM-only or vLLM plus SGLang (LiveKit runs SGLang and the hint RFCs are SGLang's); own connector versus a policy plugin on vLLM's OffloadingConnector versus waiting on the TieringManager RFC versus a fork; which idle regime to sell first (the measured body of the distribution is seconds to minutes; the priced regime is hours); the SLA gate (300 ms p95 from the repo PRD, or Twilio's 375 ms target and 750 ms maximum); the ship threshold (2x pre-registered, 1.5x measured, the repo's own tables disagree); the proof rig (7B on an L40S, which exists, or 26 to 35B on an H100, which is what buyers run); router form factor (standalone plus an llm-d plugin, vLLM production-stack, Dynamo's router, or affinity headers only); whether the hint protocol is an open spec aligned to SGLang's vocabulary or proprietary; NVMe now or after the RAM-tier win is banked; whether to publish the losses (dexa's connector, arm C) alongside the win.

### D. Document-VLM inference provider

**Thesis.** Serve an open VLM at a measured accuracy-per-dollar operating point for document pages, billed per page like the OCR bill the buyer already pays, and sold on a same-pages, rerunnable benchmark against cloud OCR, VLM parsers, and frontier models.

**Evidence.** Grade C after verification. The technical lever (2.7x throughput at a 1-point loss from 1536px to 1024px) was measured once in-engine, on 200 DocVQA pages, one run, relaxed match, as an offline eager batch with 32-token outputs, and it is a resize any client applies before upload or any self-hoster sets with a flag; against a same-config vLLM or SGLang the engine advantage is 1.0x by construction, and no measurement against vLLM's default resolution exists. The accuracy half (0.925 vs 0.880) is a 200-page head-to-head against a 2024 model whose run output was never recorded beyond that number; the loss at the operating point is 2 pages and the GPT-4o gap is 9. The cost half has never been measured in dollars: the "40x" is a pricing model whose formula contradicts the repo's own measured prompt sizes (1,017 and 2,201 tokens including question and template, not 322 visual tokens) and collapses to about 9 to 10x at the same assumed rates; the $0.02 to 0.06 per thousand pages is a hard-coded string; the deployed endpoint's pixel budget lands a full-resolution page at about 1,312 tokens, an unmeasured point between the two measured ones. The workload measured (32-token answers) is not the transcription and extraction workload the pricing pages describe (1,500 to 3,000 output tokens per page), where the visual-token lever shrinks only prefill. The intended moat, in-model visual-token pruning, is blocked by Qwen2.5-VL's positional encoding, runs on the HF path rather than vLLM, and measures accuracy only. Stated honestly, D today is a configuration-and-billing product with a benchmark, not an engine product.

**Proof benchmark.** Accuracy per dollar per page on at least 1,000 pages with ANLS (the metric vendor tables use, stricter than the repo's relaxed match) and olmOCR-Bench, every system on the same pages the same day, dollars computed from GPU-hours consumed and incumbents' returned usage counters. Baselines: vanilla vLLM at default resolution (the one that decides whether there is a product beyond a config flag), SGLang, Qwen3-VL-8B, GPT-4o, gpt-5.x, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Claude Haiku, Azure, Google, Textract, Reducto r-1, Mistral OCR.

**Reused.** `serve/modal_doc_vlm_serve.py`, the gateway and control plane, both VLM eval scripts. **New:** ANLS scorer, incumbent adapters, PDF rasterizer, page-budget policy, batch queue, per-page meter, streaming, and the six-week-late moat spike.

**Kill gate.** Qwen minus the current frontier cheap tier under 1 point ANLS on 1,000 pages (GPT-4o is a 2024 comparator and vendor tables already put open 8B models above GPT-5 on DocVQA, so the metric may be saturated); or, since a same-config vLLM matches the operating point by construction, design partners do not convert on operating point plus per-page metering plus the benchmark alone; or transcription-length outputs erase the cost effect of the visual-token lever; or a cheap tier (gpt-5.6-luna, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, DeepSeek V4-flash-vision) is within 1 point at under 2x the measured price. The verifier's estimate is 3 to 5 weeks to the benchmark proof (vendor accounts are the slip risk) and 8 to 10 weeks to a sellable metered endpoint.

**Decisions it forces.** Whether the proof must show an engine advantage at all, or whether operating point plus metering plus benchmark is the product; the proof metric (DocVQA ANLS, which may be saturated, versus olmOCR-Bench or OmniDocBench transcription or a design partner's extraction F1); the accuracy bar (parity with GPT-4o, the only measured comparator, or with today's cheap tiers); whether a per-page market is worth entering; QA/extraction versus full transcription, and which transcription model if so; Qwen2.5-VL-7B versus Qwen3-VL-8B versus a document fine-tune; per page, per token, or per GPU-hour dedicated (Gemini's fixed 560-token page and every OCR vendor already bill per page); the moat spike before, after, or never; GPU class and cloud (A100 at $1.39 to $2.50 moves the modeled floor by about 2x); concurrency target and SLA (Reducto sells 200 to 500+ concurrent pages; the endpoint runs 32 inputs per container with scale-to-zero); zero-data-retention versus keeping customer pages for the benchmark corpus; whether DeepSeek's 384-token vision model is an adjacency that matters.

### E. Verifier-guided search inference for code

**Thesis.** An API that takes a prompt plus the customer's tests, runs a continuous-batch verify-abort-refill scheduler inside the engine, and returns the first passing sample; the buyer pays for the solve, not for sixteen tries.

**Evidence.** Grade C after verification. The token saving alone would grade A (identical pass@16 at about 2.6x fewer decode tokens, reproduced three ways in-engine on HumanEval with an 8B model; 93 to 96% of the ceiling kept with a three-assert verifier, post-hoc), but that is exactly the part the repo labels buyer-replicable. Everything the product needs beyond it is bounded, contradicted, or unmeasured: the 2.12x wall-clock is a single seed and is confounded by the naive arm verifying 2,624 samples synchronously inside the step loop (the clean GPU-only number is 1.53x, "directional"); on an idle SGLang GPU early stop is 1.00 to 1.02x GPU-seconds against prefix caching; under load 1.38 to 1.87x with a simulated verifier; the in-engine-versus-client-loop delta was never measured under controlled conditions (the repo's client-loop proxy is discrete rounds, a weak baseline, and a buyer can open N streaming requests and close the losers on the first pass); nothing above max_model_len 4096, above 8B, off A100, or on a current vLLM. The measured structural fact that a client loop cannot copy: an 8-way branch at 32k cost 1.02x the GPU-seconds of one cached turn on SGLang, so the prefix can be prefilled and billed once per solve rather than once per sample. No research file documents a coding-agent product running best-of-N with tests at serving time; the documented many-samples-plus-verifier workload is RL rollouts (Fireworks rollout docs, Cursor's RL infrastructure, Relace on verl).

**Proof benchmark.** Equal pass@N at fewer tokens, fewer GPU-step seconds, and less end-to-end wall-clock on a 100-task SWE-bench-Verified subset with real sandboxed tests, verification off the GPU critical path and its latency logged, three seeds, three arms (naive; a streaming HTTP client loop that closes losers on pass; in-engine), on Llama-3.1-8B and a 30B-A3B model, at 32 concurrent solves. The verifier's estimate is 7 to 9 weeks, the SWE-bench sandbox harness being the long pole with no repo precedent; a HumanEval-only page with the verification decomposition is about 2 weeks but does not support the repo-level or larger-model claims.

**Reused.** The three verifier eval scripts (the scheduler loop is 194 lines), the gateway and control plane, the voice load harness. **New:** a sandboxed verifier pool, per-solve billing, a long-running service, streaming.

**Kill gate.** Under 1.5x token reduction on repo-level tasks; or in-engine adds under 1.15x wall-clock over a streaming client loop with close-on-pass (then ship it as an open scheduler or SDK, not a hosted engine; the verifier rates this attack fatal because it has never been measured); or verify latency erodes the wall-clock win below 1.3x; or the 2.12x does not survive moving verification off the step loop; or B independent 120k-prefix branches do not fit the GPU's HBM at the concurrency the price assumes.

**Decisions it forces.** Whether to accept the "buyer-replicable" verdict as disqualifying or as the location of the gate; which buyer (serving-time coding agents, for which no best-of-N-with-tests workload is documented, or RL-rollout inference, the only documented one); per-solve, per-token, or savings-share pricing, and whether to bill the shared prefix once per solve, the one pricing capability a client loop cannot replicate; HumanEval first (measured, weak) or SWE-bench first (unmeasured, credible); vLLM's in-process engine loop or SGLang, where the load and long-prefix measurements were taken; the GPU class for the proof; whether Morph, Relace, and Cognition adjacency matters.

### F. Portable, governed KV state layer for self-hosters (open-core)

**Thesis.** The original dexa README: the KV cache as a durable, portable, versioned, governed object the operator owns. Sessions survive a pod restart, a replica move, or a spot preemption and resume elsewhere bit-identically; state is keyed to exact weights and tenant; retention, audit, and eviction are policy, not the engine's LRU. Apache-2.0 core, a commercial fleet control plane. Sold to platform teams, neoclouds, and regulated deployments running vLLM or SGLang.

**Evidence.** Grade C after verification. Portability is measured but thin: cross-instance resume with prefill skipped is recorded at TP=1, with identical output on OPT-125m over two blocks and 8,176 of 8,192 tokens on Qwen3-0.6B at 8k; the lossless 8k-to-64k result on an 8B model is HF-level, not in vLLM. Every speed number is contradicted (0.37x single-request and 1.6 to 2.2x worse p99 under load for the repo's own connector, against 10 to 17x for LMCache single-request). Governance (RBAC, audit, retention, tenant keying) has no code, and Mooncake and Dynamo already ship tenant isolation, so the undocumented gap narrows to an audit log of state operations. Neither connector saves decode-token KV, so "zero recompute" is not a current capability. Whole-prompt keying got zero hits on prefix-sharing traffic. TP>1 and chunked prefill are unimplemented. The verifier puts the first credible public proof at about 10 weeks at TP=1 with two engineers, not 6; a TP=2 or TP=4 point adds 3 to 4 weeks.

**Proof benchmark.** A preemption-survival score: kill an instance mid-session with no drain, reschedule on another, and report KV-byte identity plus greedy divergence against a vanilla prefix-hit control (the voice repo's correctness suite refuses exact text match because cached and full-prefill kernel paths differ numerically), tokens re-prefilled per resume including decode tokens (the block-alignment floor is 16), and resume TTFT versus cold re-prefill at 8k to 128k. Baselines configured to survive: vanilla vLLM, vLLM's OffloadingConnector with an object-store tier, LMCache as a daemon with a remote L2, Mooncake Store, llm-d P2P. Survival itself is not unique to this design; the differentiation has to show up in re-prefilled tokens, correctness, and p99 under load.

**Reused.** The dexa connector (917 lines), the blob format, the voice connector's block-chain keying and slab transfer, the conformance and cross-instance test scripts. **New:** block-hash keying, chunked-prefill save, the object-store tier, TP>1 (the long pole), and every governance feature.

**Kill gate.** Resume from a durable tier loses to re-prefill at 128k; or greedy identity at 32k to 128k is no better than vanilla's own prefix-hit path; or p99 under load is more than 1.2x vanilla with loads on; or design-partner interviews find no self-hoster who loses KV to restarts (the pain quotes in the brief are hosted-API TTL pain, not self-hoster preemption evidence).

**Decisions it forces.** Whether LMCache, Mooncake, KVBM, and vLLM's in-tree offloading, all on the same connector seam, define the baselines or the exit; hosted control plane versus pure OSS (the README rules out the SaaS that `dexa_platform` and `edge` build); whether to contribute to vLLM's TieringManager RFC or differentiate out-of-tree; model class and TP scope (all evidence is dense GQA at TP=1, while the models customers name are MoE, MLA, or hybrid at TP>1, and both connectors assume a non-MLA layout); whether to own the durable store or build on LMCache's server, Mooncake Store, or NIXL and sell only the policy and control plane.

### G. Cartridges: the context compiler

**Thesis.** Compile a large static corpus into a small trained KV artifact that loads into vLLM as a precomputed prefix: 50 to 100x less memory than the full-context KV, beyond the model's context window, portable and versionable, winning for high-reuse corpora past a break-even number of queries.

**Evidence.** Grade D, confirmed by the verifier. The artifact format, the KL-distillation compiler, and a three-way benchmark harness exist and their unit tests pass (on a tiny random model, on CPU); the one quality attempt, on a 360M model on CPU, did not beat no-context; the make-or-break GPU run with corpus-conditioned synthetic Q&A was never done; high-ratio compaction lost multi-fact fidelity in the Attention Matching results, where the strongest training-free method at 128x was SnapKV at 0.86, not H2O, and those scores are a recall metric that the cartridge harness does not use (it scores token-F1 on generated answers), so the bar has to be rerun under one metric; the vLLM prefix-injection path raises an error today, and the compiler trains query positions starting at the logical corpus length, which vLLM's connector API cannot express, so the choice is a model-runner patch (a version-pinned fork) or retraining at contiguous positions. The RAG baseline in the repo is TF-IDF, not the BM25-plus-dense retriever a buyer runs. "Beyond the window" has a method gap: the teacher is a full-corpus HF prefill that cannot see past the window either. The budget is not tens of GPU-hours: the harness compiles one cartridge per example per ratio, so a LongBench-shaped run is hundreds to 1,350 compiles, roughly $1k to $5k by the repo's own cost proxy.

**Proof benchmark.** The pre-registered thresholds in `docs/BENCHMARK.md`: at 16 to 50x compression, F1 within 2 points of full context, 5 points above the best training-free compactor, 3 points above RAG, on a public corpus-QA set, with the break-even query count published.

**Kill gate.** The GPU self-study run fails the F1 thresholds on a 7 to 8B model under one metric against SnapKV, H2O, and a BM25-plus-dense RAG baseline; that single experiment is the whole decision. The verifier's estimate is about three weeks to the HF-level answer, either sign, and 7 to 9 weeks to a credible public proof (frontier plot, in-engine parity after position retraining, measured compile cost, memory against fp8 full-KV in LMCache's CPU tier) if it passes.

**Decisions it forces.** Whether to spend $1k to $5k of GPU on the make-or-break run before anything else, given that it is the only candidate with a negative data point and no in-engine evidence; benchmark shape (LongBench one-document-per-example, standard but one compile per example, or few corpora with many questions, product-shaped and cheaper); position layout (patch vLLM's model runner or retrain at contiguous positions); the loader substrate (the repo connector, a backend for vLLM's OffloadingConnector, or an LMCache tier); RAG baseline strength; where the artifact, harness, and loader go if the run fails.

### H. Computer-use agent inference endpoint

**Thesis.** An OpenAI-compatible endpoint for screenshot-heavy agents: an open VLM at a screenshot-sized visual-token budget, exact-repeat frame dedup, session affinity so the static prefix stays warm, per-request "what this step cost here versus the frontier model" telemetry, hosted or BYOC. The gateway and control plane already exist.

**Evidence.** Grade D after verification (the measured items are inputs to the thesis, not the thesis). The image-token arithmetic is a deterministic formula (322 Qwen2.5-VL tokens vs. 1,105 to 1,334 on frontier tokenizers for a 1280x800 frame) with stale comparators: re-based on current rules and prices the gap is about 30x against Sonnet 5 and 35x against gpt-5.4, but only about 3.7x against gpt-5.6-luna or gpt-5.4-nano, and 1.3 to 3.7x against frontier small-image modes (Gemini 3 low at 280 tokens, DeepSeek at 384). The accuracy evidence is on documents at 1,017 tokens, not screenshots; no grounding number exists at the served budget, and the nearest document budgets lost 20 and 49 points. The 1.05 MP budget does not change the token count for a 1280x800 frame at all, so the vanilla-vLLM baseline is token-identical there. The exact-frame cache is near-inert as coded (it fires only when the frame is byte-identical and the whole message text hash matches, which an append-only agent loop never satisfies); session affinity is unbuilt and its value rests on a text-model, single-request, in-GPU cache hit. The 322-token figure is Qwen2.5-VL's resize geometry; per-image counts for Qwen3-VL and Browser Use's 30B model are unrecorded. The redundancy numbers come from one synthetic 15-step trajectory. The delta-perception moat is bounded at 2.3x on 8 frames and blocked; no OSWorld or WebArena validation exists; the "CUA-tuned backend" is stock vLLM flags. The verifier's estimate is 7 to 9 weeks to a credible public proof, the browser-suite harness being the slip risk.

**Proof benchmark.** Dollars per completed task and p50 step latency at matched success on WebArena or Odysseys with a browser harness (Browser Use or Skyvern; OSWorld is a desktop suite that needs its own harness), five seeds, five backends, two screen sizes (at 1280x800 the cap is inert and the page should say so; at 1920x1080 the cap is the test of whether grounding survives 312 tokens), billed tokens read from the engine's usage fields, trajectories published.

**Kill gate.** Task success more than 8 points below the same model at native resolution at 1080p (the verifier's first gate, reviewed by week 3 before any router work); or cost per task within 1.5x of a hosted Qwen3-VL-8B endpoint, which leaves a Qwen host with telemetry and BYOC, an outcome the founder must decide is or is not a product rather than the brief deciding it.

**Decisions it forces.** Whether the "cheaper-VLM reseller" framing the findings memo rejected is an acceptable wedge; which suite and harness pairing counts as proof; screen-size policy (cap 1080p screens to about 312 tokens with unmeasured grounding, or serve native with no budget advantage); which comparators the telemetry publishes; whether frontier small-image modes are benchmark arms; model choice, including a CUA-trained model, with the per-frame token count re-measured for each; scale-to-zero versus always-warm replicas; per step, per token, or $0-cached pricing, and whether to license the BYOC recipe separately; whether to fund the 2.3x-ceiling delta-perception engine.

### I. The Morph playbook: one specialized model plus a purpose-built engine

**Thesis.** Pick a narrow, high-volume agent task whose output is mostly a copy of its input, train a small model plus a task-shaped speculator, and build the engine around it. The brief scored six tasks (document parse, voice turn, compaction, trace classifiers, code search, screenshot diff, tool routing) on output predictability, ledger evidence, data availability, and engine-specialization room, and selected **document page to structured text and fields** ("Fast Parse"): the output is largely verbatim page text (the speculator's draft), it is the one task with a measured model-side result in the repos, and public benchmarks, Apache-2.0 base models, and a published synthetic-data recipe exist.

**Evidence.** Grade C after verification. The one in-engine measurement that touches the workload is real but bounded: 2.7x throughput at a 1-point loss from a resize the ledger says a client can apply before any API, measured as one offline batch of 200 images at 32 output tokens, while the product's output is 300 to 800 tokens per page where decode dominates and nothing full-page has been measured. The accuracy parity (0.925 vs 0.880) is ledger-status BOUNDED: a 2024 comparator, output unrecorded except the number, one run. Every Morph-specific mechanism (copy fraction, copy speculation, a trained draft head, content-aware pruning, the fine-tune, behavior under concurrency) is unmeasured in both repos, and neither repo contains a training result. Whether vLLM's speculative-decoding path accepts Qwen3-VL image inputs is not covered by any research file; if it does not, week 2 becomes engine work of unknown length. Off-the-shelf n-gram drafts already ship in Baseten, Together, and vLLM, so the proprietary increment has to come from a trained draft head. The verifier's estimate is 8 to 10 weeks to a credible engine-only proof on a stock Qwen3-VL-8B, and 14 to 18 weeks if the fine-tuned model is part of the proof (about 50k synthetic pages from a frontier teacher at roughly $0.12 per page, with terms-of-service exposure, and three weeks rather than one).

**Proof benchmark.** Pages per GPU-second at a fixed accuracy floor and single-page p50/p95 latency, vs. vanilla vLLM and SGLang on the same base model, hosted Qwen3-VL endpoints, frontier VLMs, and the specialist parsers (Reducto r-1, Mistral OCR 4, olmOCR-2, dots.mocr). Pre-registered: at least 3x pages per GPU-second at under 1 point loss against a *well-configured* vLLM baseline (same max_pixels, FP8, prefix caching, built-in n-gram speculation), so the buyer-replicable resize does not count toward the target, and at least 2x single-stream latency from copy-speculation over that baseline. Accuracy bars: Nanonets OCR-3 at 87.4 and Mistral OCR 4 at 85.2 on olmOCR-Bench, and current frontier models rerun on the same pages, since the only comparator measured so far is GPT-4o from 2024.

**Kill gate.** Under 40% of output tokens are verbatim in the source (speculation cannot pay); or a well-configured vLLM with n-gram speculation captures most of the gain and the trained draft head adds little over it (the verifier's combined fatal case: the purpose-built engine then has no measured advantage); or copy-speculation gives under 1.5x; or vLLM's speculative path cannot take image inputs without engine surgery; or the fine-tune does not beat the base model and the published olmOCR-Bench bars.

**Decisions it forces.** The task itself (the brief's scoring of seven Morph-style tasks is a judgment, not a measurement); parse-first or extract-first; which base (Qwen3-VL-8B, 4B, 2B, dots.mocr, olmOCR-2); prompt-lookup only or a trained draft head; vLLM, SGLang, or a fork as the engine base; the synthetic-data teacher (frontier model with terms-of-service exposure, open teacher, or human-labeled subset); which accuracy metric buyers are shown (relaxed-match DocVQA, ANLS, olmOCR-Bench, or a schema-extraction eval); engine-only proof first or engine plus fine-tune; whether to publish the harness openly; whether Reducto's $0.01 per page and open OCR models are facts or verdicts.

### J. Preemptible-GPU inference made safe by portable session state

**Thesis.** Run long-lived agent sessions on spot GPUs (57 to 81% off list on H100/B200 at hyperscalers) because portable KV state turns a preemption into a restore on another instance instead of a lost session. The value at agent-scale context is continuity and fleet capacity, not first-token latency.

**Evidence.** Grade C, leaning D on everything specific to J. Portability is measured at TP=1 (identical output on a 125M model; 8,176 of 8,192 tokens on a 0.6B model at 8k; the 8B losslessness is HF-level). An off-instance tier *was* measured, and lost: the Modal volume shared across containers delivered 0.6 to 0.9 GB/s, 0.37x re-prefill at 8k single-request and 1.6 to 2.2x worse p99 under 16 to 24-way concurrency. What has never been measured is a fast durable tier (S3-class object storage or remote NVMe) into vLLM's paged KV at 64k to 128k; the derived requirement is roughly 0.5 GB/s (against eager prefill) to 1.7 GB/s (linear from the 8k point) per request, and the LMCache paper's own limit puts remote-storage loading ahead of prefill only above about 256k tokens at 32 Gbps. Chunked-prefill persistence is missing, so 64k+ sessions cannot even be saved today; decode-token KV is never saved, so resumes happen at turn boundaries. The context sizes the thesis targets (115k to 195k) are frontier-model traces; the only open-weight agent guidance in the research files is 22k to 32k, below the crossover of the measured disk path. The spot discount is class-specific and reference-dependent: 62 to 81% against hyperscaler list on H100 and B200, 16 to 19% on L40S and A100 at AWS, zero on Azure H200, and roughly zero against neocloud on-demand (hyperscaler H100 spot at $2.37 to $2.59 sits near the neocloud median of $3.39 and above Vast at $1.73). Both connectors are TP=1 and non-MLA; the models named for this buyer (GLM, Kimi, DeepSeek) are MLA at TP=8. The verifier's estimate of a credible public kill-and-resume benchmark at TP=1 is 8 to 10 weeks, not 6; the six-week plan assumed the S3 backend, the harness generalization, and the vLLM rebase were free.

**Proof benchmark.** Kill-and-resume: N concurrent agent sessions replayed from a trace; SIGKILL instance A at a random point; measure resume TTFT on B, GPU-seconds to resume, output identity, and sessions sustained per GPU, at 8k to 128k, against re-prefilling from the message log (the buyer's own fallback), LMCache with an S3 backend, vLLM's OffloadingConnector object tier, and llm-d P2P pull.

**Kill gate.** Durable-tier restore under 0.5 GB/s per request at 128k (below the floor derived from vLLM's eager prefill rate; the linear-from-8k floor is about 1.7 GB/s); or resume slower than re-prefill at 128k; or a 16-session resume storm slower than 2x a single resume; or the spot discount on the chosen class and reference price does not exceed restore plus re-placement plus interruption loss; or design-partner context histograms show open-weight agent steps below the crossover. The verifier rates two attacks fatal: the measured off-instance tier already lost, and a stateless session-affinity router that resends the message log delivers continuity and fleet capacity with no KV portability at all, leaving identical continuation as the only delta below the crossover.

**Decisions it forces.** GPU class, cloud, and the reference price for the spot delta (hyperscaler list, neocloud on-demand, or hosted dedicated endpoints at $3.99 to $8.00 per GPU-hour); the notice window (0 seconds to 2 minutes); TP=1 models versus the TP=8 MLA models where public agent traffic lives; whether continuity-only (affinity router plus transcript replay plus deterministic continuation) is a product if the tier gate fails; a custom connector versus building on LMCache 0.5.x's S3, GDS, and NIXL backends or vLLM's OffloadingConnector object tier; the frontier-trace context regime or the open-weight 32k regime as the first segment; whether a design-partner pilot gates the benchmark or follows it, since no partner is named in any file.

### K. SessionBench plus a resident-slot SLA (the wildcard)

**Thesis.** A benchmark-first company. Publish an open, reproducible multi-turn session-serving benchmark that reports sessions-per-GPU at a p95 first-token SLO with collapse detection over a 40-minute full-load window, a number no engine, provider, or platform publishes; ship the residency runtime that wins it; and sell warm session-slots with a stated retention window, billed per slot-hour by tier, upstream to platforms and providers in the Wafer motion (engagements and dedicated endpoints). Engine-neutral: the benchmark scores LMCache, vLLM's OffloadingConnector, SGLang HiCache, and Dynamo KVBM alongside the company's runtime.

**Evidence.** Grade C, confirmed by the verifier. The harness exists and ran 287,015 archived in-engine turns (recomputed), and it caught a depth-triggered collapse a ten-minute load test would miss; every headline is single-seed, synthetic-trace, one 46 GB GPU, a 7B fp16 model, vLLM 0.9.2, with server metrics unarchived and every manifest's git SHA recorded as unknown. The runtime's win is the same 1.5x single-seed result as B, and it fails the brief's own six-of-seven-bucket criterion (three full-load buckets at 311.8, 306.5, and 303.9 ms); the policy's whole measured contribution is 44.6 ms of p95 at one SLO, so at 350 to 400 ms the ratio is 1.0x. The retention SLA's honoring mechanism at scale is the RAM-restore arm that failed the gate without hints. Every 2026 OSS baseline (vLLM's OffloadingConnector, LMCache 0.5.4, SGLang HiCache, Dynamo KVBM), the H100 point, real traffic, and customer-class models are unmeasured, and mixing engine versions across arms would confound the leaderboard, so the connector port has to precede a credible v0. The slot economics are modeled off list L40S rates ($0.0026 to $0.0075 per slot-hour at N=300); DeepInfra's explicit retention multipliers and Managed Agents' $0.08 per session-hour are the comparables customers will use; whether platforms will pay per slot-hour is untested. The verifier's estimate is 8 to 10 weeks to a credible public benchmark on synthetic traces (three seeds, L40S and H100, all arms on vLLM 0.28, about 250 GPU-hours) and 12 to 16 weeks on partner transcripts.

**Proof benchmark.** The benchmark is the product: N-star (largest concurrent session count at full-load-window p95 under the SLO, median of three seeds) published as an SLO curve from 200 to 750 ms rather than a single 300 ms number, plus cost per resident slot-hour, on 46 GB and 80 GB GPUs, voice and agent trace profiles, all arms on one engine version, with archived server metrics and restore-correctness output, raw logs, and the twelve harness guards the voice repo documented. Hosted endpoints, if listed, appear on a separate knee-at-RPM table.

**Kill gate.** Any 2026 OSS stack already holds N=300 at 300 ms out of the box (then the runtime leg becomes policy-only and K still owns the standard and the retention policy); or the 1.5x is seed noise (median under 1.2x); or the policy's advantage vanishes on the SLO curve above 300 ms; or an 80 GB GPU or FP8 KV moves the baseline wall past voice-realistic N; or fewer than 2 of 10 platforms will run the benchmark on their own stack within the first quarter.

**Decisions it forces.** Neutral benchmark versus marketing for the runtime, and whether publicly scoring OSS stacks beside the runtime hands competitors the result; the SLO threshold that defines the benchmark (300 ms from the PRD, Twilio's 375 and 750, LiveKit's reported 192) and the pass criterion and aggregation window, since each choice moves every N; the target ratio (the draft's 1.4x sits below the PRD's 2x line); guarantee-with-credits versus best-effort-with-metrics retention, and single-tenant versus shared slots; open-source the harness or not; upstream (platforms and providers) versus downstream (builders); engagements plus dedicated endpoints (the Wafer motion) versus BYOC software versus an open harness plus paid certification; the v0 rig (7B fp16, or the 26 to 35B and MoE models platforms run); public traces (Mooncake, TraceLab) or partner transcripts for the first publication; whether SessionBench checks output correctness at all (the harness measures serving behavior only); one product first or three at once.

### 6.1 Observations that follow from the table, without ranking

K's harness exists, but a leaderboard that does not confound engine versions needs the connector port first, so the verifier puts a credible three-seed, two-GPU benchmark at 8 to 10 weeks; B is four weeks to a 7B three-seed replication and 10 to 14 to a proof with a 26 to 35B model, modern stock baselines, and real transcripts. The slowest are F (about ten weeks at TP=1 by the verifier's estimate) and C, because both need the connector hardened for chunked prefill, decode-KV save, and eventually TP>1 before their benchmark can even run.
- **Two candidates would be settled by a single experiment.** G by the GPU self-study run; J by the fast-durable-tier restore-bandwidth measurement (plus two design-partner context histograms, which cost no GPU time).
- **The 80 GB GPU question touches five candidates.** B, C, K, and the voice half of A and J all rest on a knee measured on a 46 GB GPU. The arithmetic says an 80 GB part moves the baseline cliff to roughly 400 to 450 sessions for the synthetic traces. Whether the residency win survives there is unmeasured and is the cheapest high-information experiment on the list.
- **Pricing shapes split cleanly.** A, C, J, K price time (residency or slots); B prices concurrency (lines); D, H, I price units of work (pages, steps, solves); E prices solves; F prices GPUs under management. Per-token providers can express none of the time-priced shapes without a residency mechanism, which is the structural argument A, C, J, and K make.

## 7. Founder decisions

Each decision states the options, what the evidence says about each (facts only), which candidates each option keeps alive, and the cheapest thing that would inform it. The judgments you reserved (market size, whether an incumbent's adjacency matters) are listed last, with the facts on both sides.

### 7.1 Workload and customer

**D1. Voice-first, agent-first, or the unified scheduler.**
Voice has the only measured knee movement (1.5x, one seed, 2.5k-token sessions, 46 GB GPU), buyers who price by concurrency, and a platform on record saying it over-provisions headroom. Agents have the larger contexts (10 to 30x restore ratios measured single-request), public telemetry on idle gaps (1.4-minute median, 20.6-minute p90, with hour-scale idle in the tail), the documented cache-miss pain (Codex on Azure, Claude Code TTL), and an unowned hint protocol, but no measured policy-versus-LRU result and no in-engine restore above 16k. Keeps alive: voice, B and K; agents, A and J; both, C. Informing experiment: the two proof benchmarks from C, weeks 2 and 4 (roughly 60 GPU-hours).

**D2. Documents, screenshots, or neither.**
Documents have the strongest accuracy evidence in the repos and a per-page unit every incumbent already bills; screenshots have the same model, a 2.3x ceiling on the intended moat, no task-suite validation, and no grounding measurement at the screenshot token budget. Keeps alive: D, I, H. Informing experiment: the 1,000-page ANLS run (D, week 1) and the copy-fraction measurement (I, week 1).

**D3. Whether to pursue the Morph playbook at all, and on which task.**
Facts: Morph's reported run rate, team size, and catalog shape; the repos contain no training result; the brief's scoring picked document parsing as the task with the most evidence and public data; voice turns scored low on output predictability. Keeps alive: I (and E as the code-side analog). Informing experiment: copy fraction on 1,000 pages (about 2 GPU-hours) and a prompt-lookup speculation test (about 5).

### 7.2 Business shape

**D4. Hosted endpoint, BYOC software, open-core, or engagements.**
Facts: the dexa README rules hosted SaaS out of scope and the platform code builds exactly that; LiveKit, Inworld, ElevenLabs, and Deepgram already self-host open models (BYOC buyers); Tensormesh sells hosted with an on-prem Operator "coming soon"; Wafer sells dedicated endpoints and engagements at $40M Series A scale with eight people; Morph sells hosted, closed, per token. Keeps alive: hosted, A, B, D, E, H, I; BYOC, B, C, K; open-core, F; engagements, C, K. Informing experiment: which of two design partners in week 6 converts, and to what.

**D5. Time-priced residency versus per-token versus per-unit-of-work.**
Facts: no first-party provider sells guaranteed pinned KV; Anthropic Managed Agents bill $0.08 per session-hour while running; Gemini bills cache storage per token-hour; Tensormesh bills cached input at $0 and plans 30% of estimated savings; DeepInfra sells 5-minute and 1-hour retention at 1.25x and 2x the input price. Keeps alive: time-priced, A, C, J, K; per line, B; per unit, D, H, I; per solve, E. Informing evidence: the metered cost per session-hour from the idle-ladder run at invoice rates.

**D6. Open-source the harness or the connector.**
Facts: Wafer publishes benchmarks openly (KernelArena) and its GPU-perf reading list has 2,271 stars; Morph's benchmark pages were unreadable in research; LMCache went OSS-first and its company raised $24.5M; the SGLang RFCs invite a hint spec. Keeps alive: an open harness strengthens K and B; an open connector strengthens F and C. Informing evidence: whether platforms require neutrality to run the benchmark on their own stack.

### 7.3 Technical bets

**D7. GPU class for the proof.**
Facts: every knee measurement is L40S (46 GB); every restore measurement is A100; both repos call an 80 GB GPU the top threat; the arithmetic moves the baseline cliff to about 400 to 450 sessions on 80 GB; H100 costs $1.73 (aggregator) to $12.29 per hour versus L40S at $0.79 to $2.25; on Modal the 96 GiB of host RAM the tiered arms used adds about 40% to the L40S price; the voice repo's own docs say "a result that vanishes on an 80 GB H100 is not a business." Keeps alive on a favorable result: B, C, K; on an unfavorable result, the voice framing narrows to a cost-per-call story on cheap GPUs. Informing experiment: one arm A and one arm D point on H100 (about 10 GPU-hours).

**D8. Model class.**
Facts: everything measured is a 7 to 8B dense model; voice platforms default to 26 to 35B or 3B-active MoEs; agent products run 30B-A3B to trillion-parameter MoEs at TP=8 where "TP=8 keeps eight copies of the KV cache"; the connector supports TP=1 only. Informing experiment: one 31B or 35B-A3B rerun of the chosen benchmark (about 20 GPU-hours).

**D9. vLLM only, vLLM plus SGLang, a policy plugin, or a fork.**
Facts: the connector API has been labeled experimental since April 2025 and the voice connector is pinned to 0.9.2 while 0.28 is current; the stock API can delay a free and manage its own tier but cannot order HBM eviction or prefetch without a request; vLLM's OffloadingConnector exposes a custom cache-policy hook and RFC #38260 proposes a TieringManager, so the residency policy could ship as a plugin or be upstreamed; SGLang has the hint RFCs and LiveKit runs it, its storage interface has no pin hook today, and the repos have no SGLang connector code; scheduler-native prefetch (the untested strong form of the voice thesis) needs a fork; Morph's Reflexes engine is "forked from vLLM" (the engine behind its other products is undisclosed). Informing evidence: the port cost from 0.9.2 to 0.28 in weeks 1 to 2.

**D10. Which experiment to fund first when they compete for the same GPU budget.**
Facts: the crossover-surface run (A, C, F, J, K) is about 40 GPU-hours; the three-seed voice replication plus H100 (B, C, K) about 35; the 1,000-page ANLS run (D, H, I) about 10; the cartridge make-or-break (G) several hundred compiles, $1k to $5k by the repo's own cost proxy; the durable-tier bandwidth test (J) about 20. A provider-TTL probe (measure when each hosted provider's cache actually expires across an idle ladder) costs about $100 of API spend and one week, and proves the pain that A, C, and J assume without proving any product's value. Two of the voice tests are lethal and cheap and run on the existing vLLM 0.9.2 code before any port: arm A on an H100 (does the wall exist on 80 GB) and a modern stock tier (vLLM's OffloadingConnector, LMCache 0.5.4) against arm D, roughly 10 GPU-hours together. Section 8 lists which paths each informs.

### 7.4 The judgments you reserved

**D11. Market size.** Not assessed anywhere in this document. The volume facts that exist: Vapi 1 to 5 million calls per day and over a billion total; ElevenLabs 10 million conversations per week; Anthropic's ~400,000 analyzed Claude Code sessions; open-weight models a majority of OpenRouter tokens; Azure and Google document-AI price tiers that break at 1 million and 5 million pages per month; Fireworks at 40 trillion tokens per day. The LLM's share of a voice minute (under 5% to 65%) determines how much spend a voice-serving product competes for; no equivalent share is published for agent products.

**D12. Whether adjacency matters, per candidate.**
- A, C, F, J, K: LMCache/Tensormesh, Mooncake, NVIDIA KVBM, llm-d, and vLLM's in-tree OffloadingConnector all sit on the same connector seam and ship LRU-family eviction; the SGLang RFCs for hints are open or inactive; Tensormesh's Operator is "coming soon." None ships return-time-aware residency, a sessions-per-GPU number, or a retention SLA.
- B: Wafer's unlabeled homepage carousel shows Vapi, Tavus, and Inworld, and it has a voice TTFT case study; LiveKit serves its own model on SGLang; no vendor advertises sessions per GPU.
- D, I: Reducto r-1 at $0.01 per page; Mistral OCR 4 at $4 per thousand; open OCR models (olmOCR-2, dots.mocr) with no hosted endpoint found; none publishes a same-pages result against open VLMs.
- E: Morph, Relace, and Cognition sell narrow code models to the same buyer; none ships a verifier-guided sampling endpoint.
- H: Anthropic's computer-use toolset, Browser Use's own 30B model, Morph's Glance; none sells a per-screenshot price or BYOC.

**D13. Whether the repos' own "go up-stack" verdict binds.** On 2026-08-02 the evals ledger concluded every path inside the inference stack was closed for a small team; on 2026-08-05 the same file concluded a stateful provider "is a business"; neither was amended and the later memo adopted the second. The first verdict's premise (KV persistence is commoditized) is what candidates A, C, F, J, and K answer with the policy layer; the policy layer is unmeasured. The second verdict's premise (the delta is the tiering policy) is designed but unenforced.

### 8.3 Path: the session engine, agent-first (A, then C or J)

## 9. What this document did not do

- It did not measure the three things the whole session family depends on: RAM-tier restore under concurrency above 16k, the residency policy against LRU, and the knee on an 80 GB GPU.

- It did not accept the briefs' first drafts. Each was fact-checked against the ledgers and attacked by a separate verifier pass; the grades in sections 5 and 6 are the verifiers', and their per-brief findings (misquoted numbers, fatal and serious attacks, decisions a draft had made on your behalf) are in `docs/product/briefs/*.revised.json`.
