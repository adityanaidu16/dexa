## 5. The eleven candidates

Each candidate has a full brief under `docs/product/briefs/` written to one template (thesis, customer, pain, proof benchmark, architecture, evidence, six-week plan, pricing, competitive facts, kill gates, founder decisions, combinations) and then fact-checked against the ledgers and attacked by a skeptic. This section is the digest. Evidence grades: **A** core claim reproduced several ways in-engine; **B** measured once in-engine or at the physics level; **C** measured but bounded or contradicted; **D** mostly design.

### A. Session-stateful inference provider for long-idle, long-context agents

**Thesis.** A durable, warm session is the primitive. Its KV is tiered HBM to RAM to NVMe by measured break-evens and priced as residency plus new tokens, so a coding or computer-use agent that idles past a provider's cache TTL restores instead of re-paying its 100k-token prefix. Sold as a hosted OpenAI-compatible endpoint with one optional `session` field.

**Evidence.** Grade C. Restore beats re-prefill in a real vLLM+LMCache stack single-request (10x at 4k, 17x at 16k); in-engine evidence stops at 16k; the RAM tier under concurrency at 16k to 64k is unmeasured; the tiering policy that is the claimed delta over LMCache is advisory in every deployed path; the cost model's "2 to 6x cheaper" is modeled at assumed rates. The customer telemetry is strong (median 115k to 126k prefixes, 1.4-minute median human gaps, 5-minute to 24-hour provider TTLs).

**Proof benchmark.** Warm-resume p95 TTFT and GPU-seconds per turn on 64k to 128k sessions across an idle ladder (10 s, 2 min, 20 min, 2 h) at 32 concurrent sessions, vs. vanilla vLLM re-prefill, LMCache LRU, vLLM's OffloadingConnector, and a hosted per-token endpoint with session affinity. This is the crossover-surface experiment from `04-conflicts.md`.

**Reused.** The session API and tier calculator in `dexa_platform/sessions`, the gateway and control plane, `serve/vllm_lmcache_backend.py`, the LMCache and warmstart eval scripts. **New:** enforced tiering, NVMe tier, chunked-prefill persistence, streaming, a session-to-replica router.

**Kill gate.** Session-aware tiering fails to beat LMCache LRU by 15% on either TTFT p95 or GPU-seconds per turn at matched memory; or restore from RAM loses to re-prefill at 64k under 32-way concurrency.

**Decisions it forces.** Voice-first or agent-first; hosted or BYOC; which GPU class the proof runs on; whether Tensormesh's $0-cached-input SaaS and the SGLang RFCs are baselines or a closing window.

### B. Conversation-native inference for voice agents

**Thesis.** The product metric is concurrent voice sessions per GPU at p95 first-token latency under 300 ms. The mechanism is the voice repo's arm D: each request carries its predicted idle window, short-idle sessions are pinned in HBM, long-idle ones evict into a contiguous pinned-RAM restore path. A vLLM connector plugin plus a session gateway; no fork. Sold to voice platforms that self-host open models (LiveKit, Inworld, ElevenLabs, Deepgram) and to their customers, hosted and BYOC.

**Evidence.** Grade C, confirmed by the verifier. The only configuration of five that moved the concurrency knee (200 to 300 on an L40S, 1.5x, with no intermediate N so the true ratio sits in [1.0x, 2.0x)); single seed; synthetic traces whose idle fraction comes from assumed distributions; 46 GB GPU; the full-load-window p95 was 292 ms with an 8 ms margin; the baselines are vanilla vLLM and a 2025 LMCache, not vLLM's native OffloadingConnector or LMCache 0.5.x; the hint is derived from the trace generator's own constants, so a shuffled-hint ablation is needed to show that prediction, rather than pinning every short reply, is what moved the knee; restores are synchronous and their latency was never timed; the connector is TP=1, fp16, non-MLA, prompt-KV only, while the platforms' documented defaults are 26 to 35B models. The repo's own thresholds conflict (README: under 2x is the kill band; RESULTS: 1.5x is above the arm-B gate); both readings are stated, neither is adopted here. LiveKit's statement that it over-provisions headroom and the per-line pricing at Vapi and Retell are the demand-side facts; no vendor page fetched advertises sessions per GPU, but that search was incomplete. The verifier's estimate of the first proof a skeptical buyer would accept (three seeds, H100, a 26 to 35B model, modern baselines, one real-transcript set, raw logs) is 10 to 14 weeks; the six-week plan below is the build schedule.

**Proof benchmark.** Max passing N at full-load-window p95 under 300 ms, median of three seeds, runs of at least 40 minutes (collapse was depth-triggered at minute 15 to 20), on L40S and on an 80 GB GPU, with a 26 to 35B model, against vanilla vLLM 0.28, vLLM's native OffloadingConnector (LRU and ARC), LMCache 0.5.4 layerwise, and SGLang HiCache, with a shuffled-hint ablation, on transcript-derived traces, with turns that never produced a first token counted as failures. Every Modal manifest today records the git SHA as unknown and no manifest names the connector or flags used; provenance is part of the deliverable.

**Reused.** `voice_kv_connector.py` (the pinning logic is about 40 lines plus a 6-line client hint), the ~2,000-line load harness, the sweep driver, the gateway. **New:** port from vLLM 0.9.2 to 0.28, async loads, proactive free of long-idle sessions, real-transcript converter, session-to-replica affinity, streaming and WebSocket transports.

**Kill gate.** Median D/A under 1.3x on three seeds; or the baseline cliff on an 80 GB GPU sits past voice-realistic session counts with D adding under 10% (the verifier rates this attack fatal if it lands; it is a week-1, roughly 10 GPU-hour test on the existing code); or a shuffled-hint arm matches D; or a modern stock tier (OffloadingConnector, LMCache 0.5.x) matches D; or real transcripts show idle fractions under 40%.

**Decisions it forces.** Cost product or latency product (the LLM is under 5% of a voice minute on some stacks and 65% on others); platforms or their customers as the buyer; whether to fork vLLM for scheduler-native prefetch; the speech-to-speech bet (continuous audio may collapse the idle window; unmeasured); the SLA number itself (300 ms p95 is the repo PRD's choice, while Twilio's published budget is 375 ms target and 750 ms maximum, and a looser SLA moves every knee); the ratio worth building on (2x pre-registered, 1.5x measured, and the repo's two thresholds disagree); vLLM-first or SGLang-first given that LiveKit, the named platform buyer, runs SGLang and SGLang's pin-with-TTL RFC is in flight; whether to run the cheap lethal tests (H100 arm A, stock OffloadingConnector) before or after the connector port; whether a design partner is a prerequisite, since none is named in any file.

### C. One session-aware residency scheduler for every idle regime

**Thesis.** The claim that unifies both repos: a scheduler that knows session semantics (predicted return time, context size, per-tier break-evens, workflow structure) beats LRU across idle windows from seconds (voice turns) to hours (human-in-the-loop agents). Built as a vLLM/SGLang connector, a policy engine, a published hint protocol, and a session router. Sold three ways: a hosted session-addressed endpoint, BYOC software for platforms, and optimization engagements for providers (the Wafer motion). The hint protocol is unowned: every shipping KV layer evicts by LRU, and the SGLang RFCs that would carry hints are open or inactive.

**Evidence.** Grade C. Same measured base as A and B (one voice win at 1.5x single seed; RAM restore 10 to 17x single-request); the agent-regime policy-versus-LRU comparison has never been run; NVMe is unmeasured; the cost model is modeled.

**Proof benchmark.** Two metrics. Metric A is B's sessions-per-GPU sweep. Metric B is A's idle ladder at 100k-token scale. Ship target: 2x on three seeds for A; a 25% improvement over LMCache LRU on TTFT p95 or GPU-seconds for B. Kill: under 1.3x and under 15%.

**Reused.** Everything A and B reuse, plus the dexa connector's async-load and adaptive-policy code and the cost model as the seed of the policy engine. **New:** the enforced policy engine, the hint spec, the router (as an llm-d endpoint-picker plugin for BYOC), NVMe, server-metric capture.

**Kill gate.** Session-aware residency beats LRU only on synthetic traces; or the connector API drifts faster than one engineer-week per release; or NVMe restore is over 3x slower than modeled.

**Decisions it forces.** Which of the three sales motions comes first; vLLM-only or vLLM plus SGLang; whether the hint protocol is an open spec or proprietary; NVMe now or after the RAM-tier win is banked.

### D. Document-VLM inference provider

**Thesis.** Serve an open VLM at a measured accuracy-per-dollar operating point for document pages, billed per page like the OCR bill the buyer already pays, and sold on a same-pages, rerunnable benchmark against cloud OCR, VLM parsers, and frontier models.

**Evidence.** Grade B for accuracy (Qwen2.5-VL-7B 0.925 vs. GPT-4o 0.880 on 200 pages, relaxed match, measured; 2.7x throughput at a 1-point loss from resolution alone); grade D for cost (the "40x" is modeled; the $0.02 to 0.06 per thousand pages is a hard-coded string; serving cost has never been measured in dollars; the accuracy was measured at 1,017 visual tokens, not the 322 the pricing model assumes). The intended moat, in-model visual-token pruning, is blocked by Qwen2.5-VL's positional encoding and has no result.

**Proof benchmark.** Accuracy per dollar per page on at least 1,000 pages with ANLS (the metric vendor tables use, stricter than the repo's relaxed match) and olmOCR-Bench, every system on the same pages the same day, dollars computed from GPU-hours consumed and incumbents' returned usage counters. Baselines: vanilla vLLM at default resolution (the one that decides whether there is a product beyond a config flag), SGLang, Qwen3-VL-8B, GPT-4o, gpt-5.x, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Claude Haiku, Azure, Google, Textract, Reducto r-1, Mistral OCR.

**Reused.** `serve/modal_doc_vlm_serve.py`, the gateway and control plane, both VLM eval scripts. **New:** ANLS scorer, incumbent adapters, PDF rasterizer, page-budget policy, batch queue, per-page meter, streaming, and the six-week-late moat spike.

**Kill gate.** Qwen minus GPT-4o under 1 point ANLS on 1,000 pages; or the operating point beats vanilla vLLM by under 1.5x pages per GPU-hour; or a frontier cheap tier is within 1 point at under 2x the measured price.

**Decisions it forces.** Whether a per-page market is worth entering; QA/extraction versus full transcription; Qwen2.5-VL-7B versus Qwen3-VL-8B versus a document fine-tune; the moat spike before or after market validation.

### E. Verifier-guided search inference for code

**Thesis.** An API that takes a prompt plus the customer's tests, runs a continuous-batch verify-abort-refill scheduler inside the engine, and returns the first passing sample; the buyer pays for the solve, not for sixteen tries.

**Evidence.** Grade C after verification. The token saving alone would grade A (identical pass@16 at about 2.6x fewer decode tokens, reproduced three ways in-engine on HumanEval with an 8B model; 93 to 96% of the ceiling kept with a three-assert verifier, post-hoc), but that is exactly the part the repo labels buyer-replicable. Everything the product needs beyond it is bounded, contradicted, or unmeasured: the 2.12x wall-clock is a single seed and is confounded by the naive arm verifying 2,624 samples synchronously inside the step loop (the clean GPU-only number is 1.53x, "directional"); on an idle SGLang GPU early stop is 1.00 to 1.02x GPU-seconds against prefix caching; under load 1.38 to 1.87x with a simulated verifier; the in-engine-versus-client-loop delta was never measured under controlled conditions (the repo's client-loop proxy is discrete rounds, a weak baseline, and a buyer can open N streaming requests and close the losers on the first pass); nothing above max_model_len 4096, above 8B, off A100, or on a current vLLM. The measured structural fact that a client loop cannot copy: an 8-way branch at 32k cost 1.02x the GPU-seconds of one cached turn on SGLang, so the prefix can be prefilled and billed once per solve rather than once per sample. No research file documents a coding-agent product running best-of-N with tests at serving time; the documented many-samples-plus-verifier workload is RL rollouts (Fireworks rollout docs, Cursor's RL infrastructure, Relace on verl).

**Proof benchmark.** Equal pass@N at fewer tokens, fewer GPU-step seconds, and less end-to-end wall-clock on a 100-task SWE-bench-Verified subset with real sandboxed tests, verification off the GPU critical path and its latency logged, three seeds, three arms (naive; a streaming HTTP client loop that closes losers on pass; in-engine), on Llama-3.1-8B and a 30B-A3B model, at 32 concurrent solves. The verifier's estimate is 7 to 9 weeks, the SWE-bench sandbox harness being the long pole with no repo precedent; a HumanEval-only page with the verification decomposition is about 2 weeks but does not support the repo-level or larger-model claims.

**Reused.** The three verifier eval scripts (the scheduler loop is 194 lines), the gateway and control plane, the voice load harness. **New:** a sandboxed verifier pool, per-solve billing, a long-running service, streaming.

**Kill gate.** Under 1.5x token reduction on repo-level tasks; or in-engine adds under 1.15x wall-clock over a streaming client loop with close-on-pass (then ship it as an open scheduler or SDK, not a hosted engine; the verifier rates this attack fatal because it has never been measured); or verify latency erodes the wall-clock win below 1.3x; or the 2.12x does not survive moving verification off the step loop; or B independent 120k-prefix branches do not fit the GPU's HBM at the concurrency the price assumes.

**Decisions it forces.** Whether to accept the "buyer-replicable" verdict as disqualifying or as the location of the gate; which buyer (serving-time coding agents, for which no best-of-N-with-tests workload is documented, or RL-rollout inference, the only documented one); per-solve, per-token, or savings-share pricing, and whether to bill the shared prefix once per solve, the one pricing capability a client loop cannot replicate; HumanEval first (measured, weak) or SWE-bench first (unmeasured, credible); vLLM's in-process engine loop or SGLang, where the load and long-prefix measurements were taken; the GPU class for the proof; whether Morph, Relace, and Cognition adjacency matters.

### F. Portable, governed KV state layer for self-hosters (open-core)

**Thesis.** The original dexa README: the KV cache as a durable, portable, versioned, governed object the operator owns. Sessions survive a pod restart, a replica move, or a spot preemption and resume elsewhere bit-identically; state is keyed to exact weights and tenant; retention, audit, and eviction are policy, not the engine's LRU. Apache-2.0 core, a commercial fleet control plane. Sold to platform teams, neoclouds, and regulated deployments running vLLM or SGLang.

**Evidence.** Grade C after verification. Portability is measured but thin: cross-instance resume with prefill skipped is recorded at TP=1, with identical output on OPT-125m over two blocks and 8,176 of 8,192 tokens on Qwen3-0.6B at 8k; the lossless 8k-to-64k result on an 8B model is HF-level, not in vLLM. Every speed number is contradicted (0.37x single-request and 1.6 to 2.2x worse p99 under load for the repo's own connector, against 10 to 17x for LMCache single-request). Governance (RBAC, audit, retention, tenant keying) has no code, and Mooncake and Dynamo already ship tenant isolation, so the undocumented gap narrows to an audit log of state operations. Neither connector saves decode-token KV, so "zero recompute" is not a current capability. Whole-prompt keying got zero hits on prefix-sharing traffic. TP>1 and chunked prefill are unimplemented. The verifier puts the first credible public proof at about 10 weeks at TP=1 with two engineers, not 6; a TP=2 or TP=4 point adds 3 to 4 weeks.

**Proof benchmark.** A preemption-survival score: kill an instance mid-session with no drain, reschedule on another, and report KV-byte identity plus greedy divergence against a vanilla prefix-hit control (the voice repo's correctness suite refuses exact text match because cached and full-prefill kernel paths differ numerically), tokens re-prefilled per resume including decode tokens (the block-alignment floor is 16), and resume TTFT versus cold re-prefill at 8k to 128k. Baselines configured to survive: vanilla vLLM, vLLM's OffloadingConnector with an object-store tier, LMCache as a daemon with a remote L2, Mooncake Store, llm-d P2P. Survival itself is not unique to this design; the differentiation has to show up in re-prefilled tokens, correctness, and p99 under load.

**Reused.** The dexa connector (917 lines), the blob format, the voice connector's block-chain keying and slab transfer, the conformance and cross-instance test scripts. **New:** block-hash keying, chunked-prefill save, the object-store tier, TP>1 (the long pole), and every governance feature.

**Kill gate.** Resume from a durable tier loses to re-prefill at 128k; or greedy identity at 32k to 128k is no better than vanilla's own prefix-hit path; or p99 under load is more than 1.2x vanilla with loads on; or design-partner interviews find no self-hoster who loses KV to restarts (the pain quotes in the brief are hosted-API TTL pain, not self-hoster preemption evidence).

**Decisions it forces.** Whether LMCache, Mooncake, KVBM, and vLLM's in-tree offloading, all on the same connector seam, define the baselines or the exit; hosted control plane versus pure OSS (the README rules out the SaaS that `dexa_platform` and `edge` build); whether to contribute to vLLM's TieringManager RFC or differentiate out-of-tree; model class and TP scope (all evidence is dense GQA at TP=1, while the models customers name are MoE, MLA, or hybrid at TP>1, and both connectors assume a non-MLA layout); whether to own the durable store or build on LMCache's server, Mooncake Store, or NIXL and sell only the policy and control plane.

### G. Cartridges: the context compiler

**Thesis.** Compile a large static corpus into a small trained KV artifact that loads into vLLM as a precomputed prefix: 50 to 100x less memory than the full-context KV, beyond the model's context window, portable and versionable, winning for high-reuse corpora past a break-even number of queries.

**Evidence.** Grade D. The artifact format, the KL-distillation compiler, and a three-way benchmark harness exist and their unit tests pass; the one quality attempt, on a 360M model on CPU, did not beat no-context; the make-or-break GPU run with corpus-conditioned synthetic Q&A was never done; high-ratio compaction lost multi-fact fidelity in the Attention Matching results; the vLLM prefix-injection path raises an error today.

**Proof benchmark.** The pre-registered thresholds in `docs/BENCHMARK.md`: at 16 to 50x compression, F1 within 2 points of full context, 5 points above the best training-free compactor, 3 points above RAG, on a public corpus-QA set, with the break-even query count published.

**Kill gate.** The GPU self-study run fails the F1 thresholds on a 7 to 8B model; that single experiment is the whole decision.

**Decisions it forces.** Whether to spend the estimated tens of GPU-hours on the make-or-break run before anything else, given that it is the only candidate with a negative data point and no in-engine evidence.

### H. Computer-use agent inference endpoint

**Thesis.** An OpenAI-compatible endpoint for screenshot-heavy agents: an open VLM at a screenshot-sized visual-token budget, exact-repeat frame dedup, session affinity so the static prefix stays warm, per-request "what this step cost here versus the frontier model" telemetry, hosted or BYOC. The gateway and control plane already exist.

**Evidence.** Grade C. The image-token arithmetic is reproducible (322 Qwen tokens vs. 1,105 GPT-4o tokens for a 1280x800 frame); the accuracy evidence is on documents, not screenshots; the "40x" is modeled; the 1.05 MP budget does not change the token count for a 1280x800 frame at all, so any Qwen host bills the same; the delta-perception moat is bounded at 2.3x and blocked; no OSWorld or WebArena validation exists; the "CUA-tuned backend" is stock vLLM flags.

**Proof benchmark.** Dollars per completed task and p50 step latency at matched success on OSWorld-Verified or a WebArena subset, five backends, billed tokens read from the engine's usage fields.

**Kill gate.** Task success more than 8 points below the same model at native resolution; or cost per task within 1.5x of a hosted Qwen3-VL-8B endpoint (then the product is a Qwen host with telemetry).

**Decisions it forces.** Whether the "cheaper-VLM reseller" framing the findings memo rejected is an acceptable wedge; which CUA suite counts as proof; whether to fund the 2.3x-ceiling delta-perception engine.

### I. The Morph playbook: one specialized model plus a purpose-built engine

**Thesis.** Pick a narrow, high-volume agent task whose output is mostly a copy of its input, train a small model plus a task-shaped speculator, and build the engine around it. The brief scored six tasks (document parse, voice turn, compaction, trace classifiers, code search, screenshot diff, tool routing) on output predictability, ledger evidence, data availability, and engine-specialization room, and selected **document page to structured text and fields** ("Fast Parse"): the output is largely verbatim page text (the speculator's draft), it is the one task with a measured model-side result in the repos, and public benchmarks, Apache-2.0 base models, and a published synthetic-data recipe exist.

**Evidence.** Grade C after verification. The one in-engine measurement that touches the workload is real but bounded: 2.7x throughput at a 1-point loss from a resize the ledger says a client can apply before any API, measured as one offline batch of 200 images at 32 output tokens, while the product's output is 300 to 800 tokens per page where decode dominates and nothing full-page has been measured. The accuracy parity (0.925 vs 0.880) is ledger-status BOUNDED: a 2024 comparator, output unrecorded except the number, one run. Every Morph-specific mechanism (copy fraction, copy speculation, a trained draft head, content-aware pruning, the fine-tune, behavior under concurrency) is unmeasured in both repos, and neither repo contains a training result. Whether vLLM's speculative-decoding path accepts Qwen3-VL image inputs is not covered by any research file; if it does not, week 2 becomes engine work of unknown length. Off-the-shelf n-gram drafts already ship in Baseten, Together, and vLLM, so the proprietary increment has to come from a trained draft head. The verifier's estimate is 8 to 10 weeks to a credible engine-only proof on a stock Qwen3-VL-8B, and 14 to 18 weeks if the fine-tuned model is part of the proof (about 50k synthetic pages from a frontier teacher at roughly $0.12 per page, with terms-of-service exposure, and three weeks rather than one).

**Proof benchmark.** Pages per GPU-second at a fixed accuracy floor and single-page p50/p95 latency, vs. vanilla vLLM and SGLang on the same base model, hosted Qwen3-VL endpoints, frontier VLMs, and the specialist parsers (Reducto r-1, Mistral OCR 4, olmOCR-2, dots.mocr). Pre-registered: at least 3x pages per GPU-second at under 1 point loss against a *well-configured* vLLM baseline (same max_pixels, FP8, prefix caching, built-in n-gram speculation), so the buyer-replicable resize does not count toward the target, and at least 2x single-stream latency from copy-speculation over that baseline. Accuracy bars: Nanonets OCR-3 at 87.4 and Mistral OCR 4 at 85.2 on olmOCR-Bench, and current frontier models rerun on the same pages, since the only comparator measured so far is GPT-4o from 2024.

**Kill gate.** Under 40% of output tokens are verbatim in the source (speculation cannot pay); or a well-configured vLLM with n-gram speculation captures most of the gain and the trained draft head adds little over it (the verifier's combined fatal case: the purpose-built engine then has no measured advantage); or copy-speculation gives under 1.5x; or vLLM's speculative path cannot take image inputs without engine surgery; or the fine-tune does not beat the base model and the published olmOCR-Bench bars.

**Decisions it forces.** The task itself (the brief's scoring of seven Morph-style tasks is a judgment, not a measurement); parse-first or extract-first; which base (Qwen3-VL-8B, 4B, 2B, dots.mocr, olmOCR-2); prompt-lookup only or a trained draft head; vLLM, SGLang, or a fork as the engine base; the synthetic-data teacher (frontier model with terms-of-service exposure, open teacher, or human-labeled subset); which accuracy metric buyers are shown (relaxed-match DocVQA, ANLS, olmOCR-Bench, or a schema-extraction eval); engine-only proof first or engine plus fine-tune; whether to publish the harness openly; whether Reducto's $0.01 per page and open OCR models are facts or verdicts.

### J. Preemptible-GPU inference made safe by portable session state

**Thesis.** Run long-lived agent sessions on spot GPUs (57 to 81% off list on H100/B200 at hyperscalers) because portable KV state turns a preemption into a restore on another instance instead of a lost session. The value at agent-scale context is continuity and fleet capacity, not first-token latency.

**Evidence.** Grade C, leaning D on everything specific to J. Portability is measured at TP=1 (identical output on a 125M model; 8,176 of 8,192 tokens on a 0.6B model at 8k; the 8B losslessness is HF-level). An off-instance tier *was* measured, and lost: the Modal volume shared across containers delivered 0.6 to 0.9 GB/s, 0.37x re-prefill at 8k single-request and 1.6 to 2.2x worse p99 under 16 to 24-way concurrency. What has never been measured is a fast durable tier (S3-class object storage or remote NVMe) into vLLM's paged KV at 64k to 128k; the derived requirement is roughly 0.5 GB/s (against eager prefill) to 1.7 GB/s (linear from the 8k point) per request, and the LMCache paper's own limit puts remote-storage loading ahead of prefill only above about 256k tokens at 32 Gbps. Chunked-prefill persistence is missing, so 64k+ sessions cannot even be saved today; decode-token KV is never saved, so resumes happen at turn boundaries. The context sizes the thesis targets (115k to 195k) are frontier-model traces; the only open-weight agent guidance in the research files is 22k to 32k, below the crossover of the measured disk path. The spot discount is class-specific and reference-dependent: 62 to 81% against hyperscaler list on H100 and B200, 16 to 19% on L40S and A100 at AWS, zero on Azure H200, and roughly zero against neocloud on-demand (hyperscaler H100 spot at $2.37 to $2.59 sits near the neocloud median of $3.39 and above Vast at $1.73). Both connectors are TP=1 and non-MLA; the models named for this buyer (GLM, Kimi, DeepSeek) are MLA at TP=8. The verifier's estimate of a credible public kill-and-resume benchmark at TP=1 is 8 to 10 weeks, not 6; the six-week plan assumed the S3 backend, the harness generalization, and the vLLM rebase were free.

**Proof benchmark.** Kill-and-resume: N concurrent agent sessions replayed from a trace; SIGKILL instance A at a random point; measure resume TTFT on B, GPU-seconds to resume, output identity, and sessions sustained per GPU, at 8k to 128k, against re-prefilling from the message log (the buyer's own fallback), LMCache with an S3 backend, vLLM's OffloadingConnector object tier, and llm-d P2P pull.

**Kill gate.** Durable-tier restore under 0.5 GB/s per request at 128k (below the floor derived from vLLM's eager prefill rate; the linear-from-8k floor is about 1.7 GB/s); or resume slower than re-prefill at 128k; or a 16-session resume storm slower than 2x a single resume; or the spot discount on the chosen class and reference price does not exceed restore plus re-placement plus interruption loss; or design-partner context histograms show open-weight agent steps below the crossover. The verifier rates two attacks fatal: the measured off-instance tier already lost, and a stateless session-affinity router that resends the message log delivers continuity and fleet capacity with no KV portability at all, leaving identical continuation as the only delta below the crossover.

**Decisions it forces.** GPU class, cloud, and the reference price for the spot delta (hyperscaler list, neocloud on-demand, or hosted dedicated endpoints at $3.99 to $8.00 per GPU-hour); the notice window (0 seconds to 2 minutes); TP=1 models versus the TP=8 MLA models where public agent traffic lives; whether continuity-only (affinity router plus transcript replay plus deterministic continuation) is a product if the tier gate fails; a custom connector versus building on LMCache 0.5.x's S3, GDS, and NIXL backends or vLLM's OffloadingConnector object tier; the frontier-trace context regime or the open-weight 32k regime as the first segment; whether a design-partner pilot gates the benchmark or follows it, since no partner is named in any file.

### K. SessionBench plus a resident-slot SLA (the wildcard)

**Thesis.** A benchmark-first company. Publish an open, reproducible multi-turn session-serving benchmark that reports sessions-per-GPU at a p95 first-token SLO with collapse detection over a 40-minute full-load window, a number no engine, provider, or platform publishes; ship the residency runtime that wins it; and sell warm session-slots with a stated retention window, billed per slot-hour by tier, upstream to platforms and providers in the Wafer motion (engagements and dedicated endpoints). Engine-neutral: the benchmark scores LMCache, vLLM's OffloadingConnector, SGLang HiCache, and Dynamo KVBM alongside the company's runtime.

**Evidence.** Grade C. The harness exists and ran 287,015 measured turns; the runtime's win is the same 1.5x single-seed result as B; the slot economics are modeled; whether platforms will pay per slot-hour is untested.

**Proof benchmark.** The benchmark is the product: N-star (largest concurrent session count at full-load-window p95 under 300 ms, median of three seeds) and cost per resident slot-hour, on 46 GB and 80 GB GPUs, voice and agent trace profiles, published with raw logs and the twelve harness guards the voice repo documented.

**Kill gate.** Any 2026 OSS stack already holds N=300 at 300 ms out of the box (then the runtime leg becomes policy-only); or the 1.5x is seed noise (median under 1.2x); or fewer than 2 of 10 platforms will run the benchmark on their own stack within six weeks.

**Decisions it forces.** Neutral benchmark versus marketing for the runtime; guarantee versus best-effort retention; open-source the harness or not; upstream (platforms) versus downstream (builders).
